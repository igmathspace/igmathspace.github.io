📖 Differential Equations – Complete Solutions
📌 Definitions & Basics
1. Define an ordinary differential equation.
Definition: An ordinary differential equation (ODE) is an equation that contains one or more derivatives of an unknown function with respect to a single independent variable. (This is the standard definition of an ODE.)
Example: \( \frac{dy}{dx} = 2x \) or \( y'' + 3y' + 2y = 0 \).
2. Find the order and degree of \( \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} + 9y = e^{-2x} \)
Order: The highest derivative is \( \frac{d^2y}{dx^2} \) (second derivative is the highest order) → order = 2
Degree: The highest derivative is raised to power 1 (no power other than 1 on the highest derivative) → degree = 1
Answer: Order = 2, Degree = 1
3. Find the order of \( \frac{d^3y}{dx^3} + 2\left(\frac{dy}{dx}\right)^{1/2} = 0 \)
Order: The highest derivative is \( \frac{d^3y}{dx^3} \) (third derivative is the highest order present) → order = 3
Note: Degree is not defined because the derivative appears under a radical (fractional power).
Answer: Order = 3
4. Verify that \( y = \frac{c}{x} \) is a solution of \( xy' = -y \)
Step 1: Given \( y = \frac{c}{x} \) (take the candidate solution)
Step 2: Differentiate: \( y' = -\frac{c}{x^2} \) (apply derivative with respect to x)
Step 3: Substitute into \( xy' = -y \): (check if the ODE is satisfied)
LHS = \( x \cdot (-\frac{c}{x^2}) = -\frac{c}{x} \) (simplify the left-hand side)
RHS = \( -y = -\frac{c}{x} \) (simplify the right-hand side)
Step 4: LHS = RHS → Verified. (the candidate satisfies the ODE)
5. Verify that \( x^2 + y^2 = c \) is a solution of \( yy' = -x \)
Step 1: Given \( x^2 + y^2 = c \) (implicitly defined solution)
Step 2: Differentiate implicitly: \( 2x + 2yy' = 0 \) (differentiate both sides with respect to x)
Step 3: \( yy' = -x \) (divide both sides by 2 to get the ODE form)
Step 4: This matches the given ODE exactly → Verified. (the implicit solution satisfies the ODE)
📝 Separable ODEs
6. Solve \( \frac{dy}{dx} = \cos x \)
Step 1: Separate variables: \( dy = \cos x \, dx \) (move all y terms to LHS, x terms to RHS)
Step 2: Integrate both sides: \( \int dy = \int \cos x \, dx \) (apply integration to both sides)
Step 3: \( y = \sin x + C \) (integrate cos x to get sin x, add constant of integration)
Solution: \( y = \sin x + C \)
7. Solve \( \frac{dy}{dx} = 1 + y^2 \)
Step 1: Separate variables: \( \frac{dy}{1 + y^2} = dx \) (move y terms to LHS, x terms to RHS)
Step 2: Integrate: \( \int \frac{dy}{1 + y^2} = \int dx \) (apply integration to both sides)
Step 3: \( \tan^{-1} y = x + C \) (integrate LHS using formula \( \int \frac{dy}{1+y^2} = \tan^{-1} y \))
Step 4: \( y = \tan(x + C) \) (take tangent on both sides to solve for y)
Solution: \( y = \tan(x + C) \)
8. Solve \( \frac{dy}{dx} = 1 + x^2 + y^2 + x^2y^2 \)
Step 1: Factor RHS: \( 1 + x^2 + y^2 + x^2y^2 = (1 + x^2)(1 + y^2) \) (factor by grouping)
Step 2: \( \frac{dy}{dx} = (1 + x^2)(1 + y^2) \) (separated form)
Step 3: Separate: \( \frac{dy}{1 + y^2} = (1 + x^2) dx \) (move y terms to LHS, x terms to RHS)
Step 4: Integrate: \( \tan^{-1} y = x + \frac{x^3}{3} + C \) (integrate both sides)
Solution: \( y = \tan\left(x + \frac{x^3}{3} + C\right) \)
9. Solve \( \frac{dy}{dx} = e^{x+y} \)
Step 1: \( \frac{dy}{dx} = e^x e^y \) (use property \( e^{x+y} = e^x e^y \))
Step 2: Separate: \( e^{-y} dy = e^x dx \) (move y terms to LHS, x terms to RHS)
Step 3: Integrate: \( -e^{-y} = e^x + C \) (integrate both sides)
Solution: \( e^{-y} = C - e^x \) or \( y = -\ln(C - e^x) \)
10. Solve \( (2 - x)dy - (3 + y)dx = 0 \)
Step 1: \( (2 - x)dy = (3 + y)dx \) (rearrange the equation)
Step 2: Separate: \( \frac{dy}{3 + y} = \frac{dx}{2 - x} \) (divide both sides appropriately)
Step 3: Integrate: \( \ln|3 + y| = -\ln|2 - x| + C \) (integrate both sides)
Step 4: \( (3 + y)(2 - x) = C \) (take exponential and simplify)
Solution: \( (3 + y)(2 - x) = C \)
11. Solve \( \frac{dy}{dx} = e^{x-y} + x^2e^{-y} \)
Step 1: \( \frac{dy}{dx} = e^{-y}(e^x + x^2) \) (factor out \( e^{-y} \))
Step 2: Separate: \( e^y dy = (e^x + x^2) dx \) (multiply both sides by \( e^y \))
Step 3: Integrate: \( e^y = e^x + \frac{x^3}{3} + C \) (integrate both sides)
Solution: \( y = \ln\left(e^x + \frac{x^3}{3} + C\right) \)
12. Solve \( \frac{dy}{dx} = \tan(x + y) \)
Step 1: Let \( u = x + y \) → \( \frac{du}{dx} = 1 + \frac{dy}{dx} \) (substitute to simplify)
Step 2: \( \frac{du}{dx} = 1 + \tan u \) (replace dy/dx with tan u)
Step 3: \( \frac{du}{1 + \tan u} = dx \) (separate variables)
Step 4: \( \frac{\cos u}{\sin u + \cos u} du = dx \) (simplify: \( 1 + \tan u = \frac{\sin u + \cos u}{\cos u} \))
Step 5: \( \frac{1}{2}\left(1 + \frac{\cos u - \sin u}{\sin u + \cos u}\right) du = dx \) (rewrite for integration)
Step 6: \( \frac{u}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\ln|\sin u + \cos u| = x + C \) (integrate both sides)
Solution: \( \frac{x+y}{2} + \frac{1}{2}\ln|\sin(x+y) + \cos(x+y)| = x + C \)
13. Solve \( \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{2}{x + 2y - 3} \)
Step 1: Let \( u = x + 2y - 3 \) → \( \frac{du}{dx} = 1 + 2\frac{dy}{dx} \) (substitute to simplify)
Step 2: \( \frac{du}{dx} = 1 + 2 \cdot \frac{2}{u} = 1 + \frac{4}{u} = \frac{u+4}{u} \) (replace dy/dx)
Step 3: \( \frac{u}{u+4} du = dx \) (separate variables)
Step 4: \( \left(1 - \frac{4}{u+4}\right) du = dx \) (rewrite the integrand)
Step 5: \( u - 4\ln|u+4| = x + C \) (integrate both sides)
Solution: \( x + 2y - 3 - 4\ln|x + 2y + 1| = x + C \) → \( 2y - 3 - 4\ln|x + 2y + 1| = C \)
📝 Linear ODEs (\( y' + p(x)y = q(x) \))
14. Verify \( y = ce^{-4x} + 0.35 \) solves \( y' + 4y = 1.4 \), find c for y(0)=2
Step 1: \( y = ce^{-4x} + 0.35 \) → \( y' = -4ce^{-4x} \) (differentiate the candidate solution)
Step 2: \( y' + 4y = -4ce^{-4x} + 4(ce^{-4x} + 0.35) = 1.4 \) → Verified. (substitute into the ODE to verify)
Step 3: Apply \( y(0)=2 \): \( 2 = c + 0.35 \) → \( c = 1.65 \) (use initial condition to find c)
Solution: \( y = 1.65e^{-4x} + 0.35 \)
15. Solve \( (1 + x^2)\frac{dy}{dx} + 2yx - 6x^2 = 0 \)
Step 1: \( \frac{dy}{dx} + \frac{2x}{1+x^2}y = \frac{6x^2}{1+x^2} \) (divide by \( 1+x^2 \) to get standard form)
Step 2: \( p(x) = \frac{2x}{1+x^2} \), \( q(x) = \frac{6x^2}{1+x^2} \) (identify p and q)
Step 3: IF = \( e^{\int \frac{2x}{1+x^2} dx} = e^{\ln(1+x^2)} = 1+x^2 \) (compute integrating factor)
Step 4: \( y(1+x^2) = \int \frac{6x^2}{1+x^2} \cdot (1+x^2) dx = \int 6x^2 dx = 2x^3 + C \) (multiply by IF and integrate)
Solution: \( y(1+x^2) = 2x^3 + C \)
16. Solve \( x \log x \, \frac{dy}{dx} + y = 2 \log x \)
Step 1: \( \frac{dy}{dx} + \frac{1}{x\log x}y = \frac{2}{x} \) (divide by \( x\log x \) to get standard form)
Step 2: \( p(x) = \frac{1}{x\log x} \), \( q(x) = \frac{2}{x} \) (identify p and q)
Step 3: IF = \( e^{\int \frac{1}{x\log x} dx} = e^{\ln(\log x)} = \log x \) (compute integrating factor)
Step 4: \( y\log x = \int \frac{2}{x} \cdot \log x \, dx \) (multiply by IF and integrate)
Step 5: \( \int \frac{2\log x}{x} dx = (\log x)^2 + C \) (integrate using substitution \( u = \log x \))
Solution: \( y\log x = (\log x)^2 + C \)
17. Solve \( x\cos x \frac{dy}{dx} + y(x\sin x + \cos x) = 1 \)
Step 1: \( \frac{dy}{dx} + \frac{x\sin x + \cos x}{x\cos x}y = \frac{1}{x\cos x} \) (divide by \( x\cos x \))
Step 2: \( \frac{x\sin x + \cos x}{x\cos x} = \tan x + \frac{1}{x} \) (simplify p(x))
Step 3: \( p(x) = \tan x + \frac{1}{x} \), \( q(x) = \frac{1}{x\cos x} \) (identify p and q)
Step 4: IF = \( e^{\int (\tan x + 1/x) dx} = e^{\ln|\sec x| + \ln|x|} = x\sec x \) (compute integrating factor)
Step 5: \( y(x\sec x) = \int \frac{1}{x\cos x} \cdot x\sec x \, dx = \int \sec^2 x \, dx = \tan x + C \) (multiply by IF and integrate)
Solution: \( yx\sec x = \tan x + C \)
18. Solve \( \frac{dy}{dx} + 2y\tan x = \sin x, \; y(\pi/3) = 0 \)
Step 1: \( p(x) = 2\tan x \), \( q(x) = \sin x \) (identify p and q)
Step 2: IF = \( e^{\int 2\tan x dx} = e^{-2\ln|\cos x|} = \sec^2 x \) (compute integrating factor)
Step 3: \( y\sec^2 x = \int \sin x \sec^2 x \, dx = \int \frac{\sin x}{\cos^2 x} dx = \sec x + C \) (multiply by IF and integrate)
Step 4: \( y = \cos^2 x(\sec x + C) = \cos x + C\cos^2 x \) (solve for y)
Step 5: Apply \( y(\pi/3)=0 \): \( 0 = \cos(\pi/3) + C\cos^2(\pi/3) = \frac{1}{2} + C \cdot \frac{1}{4} \) → \( C = -2 \) (use initial condition)
Solution: \( y = \cos x - 2\cos^2 x \)
19. Solve \( \cos^2 x \frac{dy}{dx} + y = \tan x \)
Step 1: \( \frac{dy}{dx} + \sec^2 x \, y = \tan x \sec^2 x \) (divide by \( \cos^2 x \))
Step 2: \( p(x) = \sec^2 x \), \( q(x) = \tan x \sec^2 x \) (identify p and q)
Step 3: IF = \( e^{\int \sec^2 x dx} = e^{\tan x} \) (compute integrating factor)
Step 4: \( y e^{\tan x} = \int \tan x \sec^2 x \, e^{\tan x} dx \) (multiply by IF)
Step 5: Let \( u = \tan x \) → \( du = \sec^2 x dx \) → \( \int u e^u du = e^u(u-1) + C \) (integrate by parts)
Step 6: \( y e^{\tan x} = e^{\tan x}(\tan x - 1) + C \) (substitute back)
Solution: \( y = \tan x - 1 + C e^{-\tan x} \)
20. Solve \( \frac{dy}{dx} + y\cot x = 4x\csc x, \; y(\pi/2) = 0 \)
Step 1: \( p(x) = \cot x \), \( q(x) = 4x\csc x \) (identify p and q)
Step 2: IF = \( e^{\int \cot x dx} = e^{\ln|\sin x|} = \sin x \) (compute integrating factor)
Step 3: \( y\sin x = \int 4x\csc x \cdot \sin x \, dx = \int 4x \, dx = 2x^2 + C \) (multiply by IF and integrate)
Step 4: Apply \( y(\pi/2)=0 \): \( 0 = 2(\pi/2)^2 + C \) → \( C = -\frac{\pi^2}{2} \) (use initial condition)
Solution: \( y\sin x = 2x^2 - \frac{\pi^2}{2} \)
📝 Exact ODEs
21. (a) Define an exact differential equation. (b) Solve \( e^{3y}(dx + 3xdy) = 0 \)
(a) Definition: An ODE \( M(x,y)dx + N(x,y)dy = 0 \) is exact if \( \frac{\partial M}{\partial y} = \frac{\partial N}{\partial x} \). (condition for exactness)
(b) Step 1: \( e^{3y}dx + 3xe^{3y}dy = 0 \) → \( M = e^{3y} \), \( N = 3xe^{3y} \) (identify M and N)
Step 2: \( M_y = 3e^{3y} \), \( N_x = 3e^{3y} \) → Exact (check exactness condition)
Step 3: \( u = \int M dx = xe^{3y} + h(y) \) (integrate M with respect to x)
Step 4: \( u_y = 3xe^{3y} + h'(y) = 3xe^{3y} \) → \( h'(y) = 0 \) → \( h(y) = C \) (compare with N to find h(y))
Solution: \( xe^{3y} = C \)
22. Solve \( 3e^x \tan y \, dx + (1 - e^x)\sec^2 y \, dy = 0 \)
Step 1: \( M = 3e^x \tan y \), \( N = (1 - e^x)\sec^2 y \) (identify M and N)
Step 2: \( M_y = 3e^x \sec^2 y \), \( N_x = -e^x \sec^2 y \) → Not exact (check exactness: \( M_y \neq N_x \))
Step 3: Find IF: \( \frac{M_y - N_x}{N} = \frac{3e^x \sec^2 y + e^x \sec^2 y}{(1 - e^x)\sec^2 y} = \frac{4e^x}{1 - e^x} \) (function of x only)
Step 4: IF = \( e^{\int \frac{4e^x}{1-e^x} dx} = e^{-4\ln|1-e^x|} = (1 - e^x)^{-4} \) (compute integrating factor)
Step 5: Multiply: \( \frac{3e^x \tan y}{(1-e^x)^4} dx + \frac{\sec^2 y}{(1-e^x)^3} dy = 0 \) (exact now)
Step 6: \( u = \int \frac{\sec^2 y}{(1-e^x)^3} dy = \frac{\tan y}{(1-e^x)^3} + h(x) \) (integrate N with respect to y)
Step 7: \( u_x = \frac{3e^x \tan y}{(1-e^x)^4} + h'(x) = \frac{3e^x \tan y}{(1-e^x)^4} \) → \( h'(x) = 0 \) (compare with M)
Solution: \( \frac{\tan y}{(1-e^x)^3} = C \)
📝 Bernoulli Equations
23. Solve \( y' = 3.2y - 10y^2 \) (Logistic equation)
Step 1: \( y' - 3.2y = -10y^2 \) (rearrange to Bernoulli form \( y' + p(x)y = g(x)y^a \))
Step 2: \( a = 2 \), \( p(x) = -3.2 \), \( g(x) = -10 \) (identify parameters)
Step 3: Let \( u = y^{-1} \) → \( u' = -y^{-2}y' \) (substitute)
Step 4: \( -u' - 3.2u = -10 \) → \( u' + 3.2u = 10 \) (linear ODE in u)
Step 5: IF = \( e^{3.2x} \) → \( u e^{3.2x} = \int 10 e^{3.2x} dx = \frac{10}{3.2} e^{3.2x} + C \) (solve linear ODE)
Solution: \( y = \frac{1}{3.125 + C e^{-3.2x}} \) (also y=0 is a solution)
24. Solve \( y - x\frac{dy}{dx} = a\left(y^2 + \frac{dy}{dx}\right) \)
Step 1: \( y - xy' = a y^2 + a y' \) (expand RHS)
Step 2: \( -xy' - ay' = a y^2 - y \) → \( -(x+a)y' = a y^2 - y \) (rearrange terms)
Step 3: \( y' = \frac{y - a y^2}{x + a} \) (solve for y')
Step 4: Bernoulli: \( y' + \frac{a}{x+a}y = \frac{1}{x+a}y^2 \) (write in standard Bernoulli form \( y' + p(x)y = g(x)y^2 \))
Step 5: \( a = 2 \), let \( u = y^{-1} \) → \( u' = -y^{-2}y' \) (substitute)
Step 6: \( -u' + \frac{a}{x+a}u = \frac{1}{x+a} \) → \( u' - \frac{a}{x+a}u = -\frac{1}{x+a} \) (linear ODE in u)
Step 7: IF = \( e^{\int -a/(x+a) dx} = e^{-a\ln|x+a|} = (x+a)^{-a} \) (compute integrating factor)
Solution: \( u(x+a)^{-a} = \int -\frac{1}{x+a}(x+a)^{-a} dx = \frac{(x+a)^{-a}}{a} + C \) → \( y = \frac{1}{1/a + C(x+a)^a} \)
📝 Homogeneous ODEs
25. Solve \( (x - y)2\frac{dy}{dx} = a^2 \)
Step 1: \( \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{a^2}{2(x-y)} \) (solve for y')
Step 2: Homogeneous type: Let \( y = vx \) → \( \frac{dy}{dx} = v + x\frac{dv}{dx} \) (substitute)
Step 3: \( v + xv' = \frac{a^2}{2x(1-v)} \) (substitute y = vx)
Step 4: \( xv' = \frac{a^2}{2x(1-v)} - v = \frac{a^2 - 2vx^2(1-v)}{2x(1-v)} \) (rearrange)
This is not homogeneous in the standard sense. Let \( u = x - y \) → \( \frac{du}{dx} = 1 - \frac{dy}{dx} \)
Step 5: \( \frac{du}{dx} = 1 - \frac{a^2}{2u} \) → \( \frac{2u}{2u - a^2} du = dx \) (separate variables)
Solution: \( u + \frac{a^2}{2}\ln|2u - a^2| = x + C \) → \( x-y + \frac{a^2}{2}\ln|2(x-y) - a^2| = x + C \)
26. Solve \( (x + y + 1)\frac{dy}{dx} = 1 \)
Step 1: \( \frac{dy}{dx} = \frac{1}{x+y+1} \) (solve for y')
Step 2: Let \( u = x + y + 1 \) → \( \frac{du}{dx} = 1 + \frac{dy}{dx} \) (substitute)
Step 3: \( \frac{du}{dx} = 1 + \frac{1}{u} = \frac{u+1}{u} \) (replace dy/dx)
Step 4: \( \frac{u}{u+1} du = dx \) → \( \left(1 - \frac{1}{u+1}\right) du = dx \) (separate variables)
Step 5: \( u - \ln|u+1| = x + C \) (integrate both sides)
Solution: \( x + y + 1 - \ln|x + y + 2| = x + C \) → \( y + 1 - \ln|x + y + 2| = C \)
📝 Substitution Methods (Reducible to Separable)
27. Solve \( \frac{dy}{dx} = (4x + y + 1)^2 \)
Step 1: Let \( u = 4x + y + 1 \) → \( \frac{du}{dx} = 4 + \frac{dy}{dx} \) (substitute)
Step 2: \( \frac{du}{dx} = 4 + u^2 \) (replace dy/dx with u²)
Step 3: \( \frac{du}{u^2 + 4} = dx \) (separate variables)
Step 4: \( \frac{1}{2}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{u}{2}\right) = x + C \) (integrate)
Solution: \( \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{4x+y+1}{2}\right) = 2x + C \)
28. Solve \( \frac{dy}{dx} = (3x + 4y + 1)^2 \)
Step 1: Let \( u = 3x + 4y + 1 \) → \( \frac{du}{dx} = 3 + 4\frac{dy}{dx} \) (substitute)
Step 2: \( \frac{du}{dx} = 3 + 4u^2 \) (replace dy/dx with u²)
Step 3: \( \frac{du}{3 + 4u^2} = dx \) (separate variables)
Step 4: \( \frac{1}{2\sqrt{3}}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{2u}{\sqrt{3}}\right) = x + C \) (integrate using formula)
Solution: \( \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{2(3x+4y+1)}{\sqrt{3}}\right) = 2\sqrt{3}x + C \)
29. Solve \( \frac{dy}{dx} = (2x + y + 1)^2 \)
Step 1: Let \( u = 2x + y + 1 \) → \( \frac{du}{dx} = 2 + \frac{dy}{dx} \) (substitute)
Step 2: \( \frac{du}{dx} = 2 + u^2 \) (replace dy/dx with u²)
Step 3: \( \frac{du}{u^2 + 2} = dx \) (separate variables)
Step 4: \( \frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\tan^{-1}\left(\frac{u}{\sqrt{2}}\right) = x + C \) (integrate)
Solution: \( \tan^{-1}\left(\frac{2x+y+1}{\sqrt{2}}\right) = \sqrt{2}x + C \)
📝 Initial Value Problems
30. Solve \( xy' + y = 0, \; y(4) = 6 \)
Step 1: \( y' = -\frac{y}{x} \) → \( \frac{dy}{y} = -\frac{dx}{x} \) (rearrange and separate variables)
Step 2: \( \ln|y| = -\ln|x| + C \) → \( y = \frac{C_1}{x} \) (integrate and take exponential)
Step 3: Apply \( y(4)=6 \): \( 6 = \frac{C_1}{4} \) → \( C_1 = 24 \) (use initial condition to find C₁)
Solution: \( y = \frac{24}{x} \)
31. Solve \( y' = 1 + 4y^2, \; y(1) = 0 \)
Step 1: Separate: \( \frac{dy}{1+4y^2} = dx \) (move y terms to LHS)
Step 2: \( \frac{1}{2}\tan^{-1}(2y) = x + C \) (integrate using \( \int \frac{dy}{1+(2y)^2} = \frac{1}{2}\tan^{-1}(2y) \))
Step 3: Apply \( y(1)=0 \): \( \frac{1}{2}\tan^{-1}(0) = 1 + C \) → \( C = -1 \) (use initial condition to find C)
Solution: \( \tan^{-1}(2y) = 2x - 2 \) → \( y = \frac{1}{2}\tan(2x - 2) \)
32. Solve \( y' + 2y = 4\cos 2x, \; y(\pi/4) = 3 \)
Step 1: \( p(x)=2 \), \( q(x)=4\cos 2x \) (identify p and q)
Step 2: IF = \( e^{2x} \) (compute integrating factor)
Step 3: \( y e^{2x} = \int 4\cos 2x \, e^{2x} dx = e^{2x}(\cos 2x + \sin 2x) + C \) (integrate using formula)
Step 4: \( y = \cos 2x + \sin 2x + C e^{-2x} \) (solve for y)
Step 5: Apply \( y(\pi/4)=3 \): \( 3 = 0 + 1 + C e^{-\pi/2} \) → \( C = 2e^{\pi/2} \) (use initial condition)
Solution: \( y = \cos 2x + \sin 2x + 2e^{\pi/2 - 2x} \)
33. Solve \( (e^{x+y} + ye^y)dx + (xe^y - 1)dy = 0, \; y(0) = -1 \)
Step 1: \( M = e^{x+y} + ye^y \), \( N = xe^y - 1 \) (identify M and N)
Step 2: \( M_y = e^{x+y} + e^y + ye^y \), \( N_x = e^y \) → Not exact (check exactness condition)
Step 3: Find IF: \( R^* = \frac{1}{M}(N_x - M_y) = -1 \) → IF = \( e^{-y} \) (compute integrating factor)
Step 4: Multiply: \( (e^x + y)dx + (x - e^{-y})dy = 0 \) (exact now)
Step 5: \( u = e^x + xy + e^{-y} = C \) (integrate to find potential function)
Step 6: Apply \( y(0)=-1 \): \( 1 + 0 + e = C \) → \( C = 1 + e \) (use initial condition)
Solution: \( e^x + xy + e^{-y} = 1 + e \)
📋 Quick Reference
  • Separable: \( g(y)dy = f(x)dx \) (separate variables and integrate)
  • Linear: \( y' + p(x)y = q(x) \) → IF = \( e^{\int p(x)dx} \) (multiply by IF and integrate)
  • Exact: \( Mdx + Ndy = 0 \), \( M_y = N_x \) → \( u = \int M dx \) (find potential function u)
  • Bernoulli: \( y' + p(x)y = g(x)y^a \) → \( u = y^{1-a} \) (substitute to get linear ODE)
  • Homogeneous: \( y' = f(y/x) \) → let \( y = vx \) (substitute and separate variables)
  • Reducible: Let \( u = ax + by + c \) (substitute to simplify)
