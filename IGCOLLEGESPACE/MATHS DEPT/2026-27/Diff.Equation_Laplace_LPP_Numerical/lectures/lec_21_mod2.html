🎯 1. Learning Outcomes (CO2)
  • State the integral theorem: \( \mathcal{L}\left\{\int_0^t f(\tau)\,d\tau\right\} = \frac{1}{s}F(s) \)
  • Prove the theorem using the derivative theorem and the growth restriction
  • Apply the theorem to find inverse transforms such as \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s(s^2+\omega^2)}\right\} \)
  • Use the theorem repeatedly to obtain \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s^2(s^2+\omega^2)}\right\} \)
📐 2. Theorem 3: Transform of the Integral of a Function
Integral Theorem

Let \( F(s) \) denote the transform of a function \( f(t) \) which is piecewise continuous for \( t \ge 0 \) and satisfies a growth restriction (2) of Sec. 6.1. Then, for \( s > 0 \), \( s > k \), and \( t > 0 \),

\[ \boxed{\; \mathcal{L}\left\{\int_{0}^{t} f(\tau)\, d\tau\right\} = \frac{1}{s}\,F(s) \; } \]

Equivalently, in inverse form:

\[ \int_{0}^{t} f(\tau)\, d\tau = \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s}\,F(s)\right\} \]
📝 3. Proof of the Integral Theorem
Step 1: Denote the integral by \( g(t) = \displaystyle\int_{0}^{t} f(\tau)\, d\tau \).
Step 2: Since \( f(t) \) is piecewise continuous, \( g(t) \) is continuous, and \( g'(t) = f(t) \) except at points of discontinuity of \( f \).
Step 3: Show \( g \) satisfies a growth restriction: \( |g(t)| \le \displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}|f(\tau)|d\tau \le M\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t} e^{k\tau}d\tau = \frac{M}{k}(e^{kt}-1) \le \frac{M}{k}e^{kt} \).
Step 4: Also \( g(0) = 0 \) (integral from 0 to 0 is zero).
Step 5: Apply the first-derivative theorem to \( g \): \( \mathcal{L}\{g'(t)\} = s\,\mathcal{L}\{g(t)\} - g(0) \).
Step 6: Since \( g'(t) = f(t) \) and \( g(0)=0 \): \( \mathcal{L}\{f(t)\} = s\,\mathcal{L}\{g(t)\} \).
Step 7: So \( F(s) = s\,\mathcal{L}\{g(t)\} \).
Step 8: Divide by \( s \): \( \mathcal{L}\{g(t)\} = \dfrac{1}{s}F(s) \).
Step 9: Taking inverse transforms gives \( \displaystyle\int_0^t f(\tau)d\tau = \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s}F(s)\right\} \). \( \blacksquare \)
⚠️ CAUTION! Integration in \( t \) corresponds to division by \( s \). The theorem holds under the growth restriction; the lower limit of the integral must be \( 0 \).
📊 4. Applications of the Integral Theorem

Example: Find \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s(s^2+\omega^2)}\right\} \) and \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s^2(s^2+\omega^2)}\right\} \)

From the table, \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s^2+\omega^2}\right\} = \dfrac{\sin\omega t}{\omega} \).
By the integral theorem: \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s(s^2+\omega^2)}\right\} = \displaystyle\int_0^t \dfrac{\sin\omega\tau}{\omega}d\tau = \dfrac{1}{\omega^2}(1-\cos\omega t) \).
Apply the theorem again: \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s^2(s^2+\omega^2)}\right\} = \displaystyle\int_0^t \dfrac{1-\cos\omega\tau}{\omega^2}d\tau = \dfrac{t}{\omega^2} - \dfrac{\sin\omega t}{\omega^3} \).

Example: Find \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s(s-a)}\right\} \) using the integral theorem.

\( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s-a}\right\} = e^{at} \).
By the integral theorem: \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s(s-a)}\right\} = \displaystyle\int_0^t e^{a\tau}d\tau = \dfrac{e^{at}-1}{a} \).

Problem 1. Find \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s(s-a)}\right\} \) using the integral theorem. CO2
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Write \( \dfrac{1}{s(s-a)} = \dfrac{1}{s}\cdot\dfrac{1}{s-a} \).
Step 2: Identify \( F(s) = \dfrac{1}{s-a} \).
Step 3: From the standard table, \( f(t) = \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s-a}\right\} = e^{at} \).
Step 4: By the integral theorem, \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s}F(s)\right\} = \displaystyle\int_0^t f(\tau)d\tau \).
Step 5: So \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s(s-a)}\right\} = \displaystyle\int_0^t e^{a\tau}d\tau \).
Step 6: Evaluate: \( = \left[\dfrac{e^{a\tau}}{a}\right]_0^t = \dfrac{e^{at}-1}{a} \).

✅ \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s(s-a)}\right\} = \dfrac{e^{at}-1}{a} \).

Problem 2. Find \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s(s^2+\omega^2)}\right\} \) and \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s^2(s^2+\omega^2)}\right\} \). CO2
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: From the standard table, \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s^2+\omega^2}\right\} = \dfrac{\sin\omega t}{\omega} \).
Step 2: Apply the integral theorem: \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s(s^2+\omega^2)}\right\} = \displaystyle\int_0^t \dfrac{\sin\omega\tau}{\omega}d\tau \).
Step 3: Evaluate: \( = \dfrac{1}{\omega}\left[-\dfrac{\cos\omega\tau}{\omega}\right]_0^t = \dfrac{1}{\omega^2}(1-\cos\omega t) \).
Step 4: Apply the integral theorem again to \( \dfrac{1}{s}\cdot\dfrac{1}{s(s^2+\omega^2)} \): \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s^2(s^2+\omega^2)}\right\} = \displaystyle\int_0^t \dfrac{1-\cos\omega\tau}{\omega^2}d\tau \).
Step 5: Evaluate: \( = \dfrac{1}{\omega^2}\left[\tau - \dfrac{\sin\omega\tau}{\omega}\right]_0^t = \dfrac{t}{\omega^2} - \dfrac{\sin\omega t}{\omega^3} \).

✅ \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s(s^2+\omega^2)}\right\} = \dfrac{1-\cos\omega t}{\omega^2} \), \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s^2(s^2+\omega^2)}\right\} = \dfrac{t}{\omega^2} - \dfrac{\sin\omega t}{\omega^3} \).

Problem 3. Find the inverse Laplace transform of \( \dfrac{1}{s^3 - s} \). CO2
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Factor \( s^3 - s = s(s^2-1) = s(s-1)(s+1) \).
Step 2: Partial fractions: \( \dfrac{1}{s(s^2-1)} = \dfrac{A}{s} + \dfrac{B}{s-1} + \dfrac{C}{s+1} \).
Step 3: Multiply by \( s(s-1)(s+1) \): \( 1 = A(s-1)(s+1) + B s(s+1) + C s(s-1) \).
Step 4: Set \( s=0 \): \( 1 = A(-1)(1) = -A \), so \( A = -1 \).
Step 5: Set \( s=1 \): \( 1 = B(1)(2) = 2B \), so \( B = \tfrac{1}{2} \).
Step 6: Set \( s=-1 \): \( 1 = C(-1)(-2) = 2C \), so \( C = \tfrac{1}{2} \).
Step 7: So \( \dfrac{1}{s^3-s} = -\dfrac{1}{s} + \dfrac{1/2}{s-1} + \dfrac{1/2}{s+1} \).
Step 8: Take inverse: \( \mathcal{L}^{-1} = -1 + \tfrac{1}{2}e^{t} + \tfrac{1}{2}e^{-t} \).
Step 9: Since \( \cosh t = \tfrac{e^t+e^{-t}}{2} \), we get \( = -1 + \cosh t \).

✅ \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s^3-s}\right\} = \cosh t - 1 \).

  3. Using the integral theorem, find \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s(s-a)}\right\} \). (6 marks)
  4. Find \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s^3-2s^2}\right\} \). (6 marks)

  6. Using the integral theorem, find \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s(s^2-4)}\right\} \). (6 marks)
📋 7. Quick Summary
  • Integral theorem: \( \mathcal{L}\left\{\displaystyle\int_0^t f(\tau)d\tau\right\} = \dfrac{1}{s}F(s) \)
  • Inverse form: \( \displaystyle\int_0^t f(\tau)d\tau = \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s}F(s)\right\} \)
  • Key idea: integration in \( t \) ↔ division by \( s \) (the dual of the derivative theorem).
  • Application: repeated use produces inverse transforms like \( \dfrac{1}{s^2(s^2+\omega^2)} \).
