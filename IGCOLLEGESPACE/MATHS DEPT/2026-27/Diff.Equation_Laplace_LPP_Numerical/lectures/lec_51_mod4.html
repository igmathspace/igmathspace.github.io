🎯 1. Learning Outcomes (CO4)
  • Derive the Newton-Raphson iteration formula using Taylor series
  • Interpret the method geometrically as a tangent-line approximation
  • Apply the algorithm to solve algebraic and transcendental equations
  • Recognise the conditions for convergence and common pitfalls
📐 2. Derivation of the Newton-Raphson Formula
📌 Derivation via Taylor Series

Let \(x_0\) be an approximate root of \(f(x) = 0\), and let \(x_1 = x_0 + h\) be the correct root, so that \(f(x_1) = 0\).

Expanding \(f(x_0 + h)\) by Taylor's series about \(x_0\):

\[ f(x_0 + h) = f(x_0) + h f'(x_0) + \frac{h^2}{2!} f''(x_0) + \dots \]

Neglecting second and higher-order derivatives (assuming \(h\) is small), we get:

\[ f(x_0) + h f'(x_0) \approx 0 \quad \Rightarrow \quad h \approx -\frac{f(x_0)}{f'(x_0)} \]

A better approximation than \(x_0\) is:

\[ x_1 = x_0 + h = x_0 - \frac{f(x_0)}{f'(x_0)} \]

Repeating this process gives the Newton-Raphson iteration formula:

\[ \boxed{x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}} \quad \text{for } n = 0, 1, 2, \dots \]
📏 3. Geometric Interpretation
📌 Tangent-Line Method

The Newton-Raphson method is also known as the tangent method. Geometrically, at the point \((x_n, f(x_n))\), we draw the tangent to the curve \(y = f(x)\).

The equation of the tangent at \((x_n, f(x_n))\) is:

\[ y - f(x_n) = f'(x_n)(x - x_n) \]

This tangent intersects the \(x\)-axis at \(x = x_{n+1}\), where \(y = 0\):

\[ 0 - f(x_n) = f'(x_n)(x_{n+1} - x_n) \]
\[ \Rightarrow \quad x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)} \]

Thus, the method replaces the curve by its tangent line at each step to approximate the root.

⚙️ 4. Algorithm (Computational Steps)

Step 1: Choose an initial approximation \(x_0\) close to the root (using sign change or graphical inspection).

Step 2: Compute \(f(x_0)\) and \(f'(x_0)\).

Step 3: Calculate the next approximation:

\[ x_1 = x_0 - \frac{f(x_0)}{f'(x_0)} \]

Step 4: Check the stopping criterion. If \(|x_1 - x_0| < \epsilon\) (or the relative error is below a tolerance), stop and declare \(x_1\) as the root. Otherwise, set \(x_0 = x_1\) and repeat from Step 2.

🚨 IMPORTANT CAUTION
  • The method fails if \(f'(x_n) = 0\) (horizontal tangent) or is very small.
  • A poor initial guess may lead to divergence or convergence to an unintended root.
  • For trigonometric functions, ensure your calculator is in RADIAN mode.

Problem 1 (B5). Use the Newton-Raphson method to find a root of the equation \(x^3 - 2x - 5 = 0\). CO4
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Let \(f(x) = x^3 - 2x - 5\). Then \(f'(x) = 3x^2 - 2\).
Step 2: Choose \(x_0 = 2\) (since \(f(2) = -1\) and \(f(3) = 16\), root lies between 2 and 3).
Step 3: Iteration 1: \(f(2) = -1\), \(f'(2) = 10\).
\[ x_1 = 2 - \frac{-1}{10} = 2.1 \]
Step 4: Iteration 2: \(f(2.1) = 0.061\), \(f'(2.1) = 11.23\).
\[ x_2 = 2.1 - \frac{0.061}{11.23} = 2.094568 \]
Step 5: Iteration 3: \(f(2.094568) \approx 0.000000\), so \(x_3 \approx 2.094568\).

✅ The root is approximately \(2.0945\).

Problem 2 (B6). Find a root of the equation \(x\sin x + \cos x = 0\). CO4
🚨 NOTE: Set your calculator to RADIAN mode before evaluating \(\sin x\) and \(\cos x\).
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Let \(f(x) = x\sin x + \cos x\). Then \(f'(x) = \sin x + x\cos x - \sin x = x\cos x\).
Step 2: Choose \(x_0 = \pi \approx 3.1416\).
Step 3: Iteration 1: \(f(\pi) = \pi\sin\pi + \cos\pi = -1\), \(f'(\pi) = \pi\cos\pi = -\pi\).
\[ x_1 = \pi - \frac{-1}{-\pi} = \pi - \frac{1}{\pi} \approx 3.1416 - 0.3183 = 2.8233 \]
Step 4: Iteration 2: \(f(2.8233) \approx -0.0662\), \(f'(2.8233) \approx -2.8233\).
\[ x_2 = 2.8233 - \frac{-0.0662}{-2.8233} \approx 2.7986 \]
Step 5: Iteration 3: \(f(2.7986) \approx -0.0006\), \(f'(2.7986) \approx -2.6273\).
\[ x_3 \approx 2.7984 \]
Step 6: Iteration 4: \(f(2.7984) \approx 0.0000\).

✅ The root is approximately \(2.7984\).

Problem 3 (C4). Explain Newton-Raphson method to compute a real root of \(f(x) = 0\). Hence find a non-zero root of \(x^2 + 4\sin x = 0\). CO4
🚨 NOTE: Set your calculator to RADIAN mode.
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Let \(f(x) = x^2 + 4\sin x\). Then \(f'(x) = 2x + 4\cos x\).
Step 2: Non-zero root: try \(x_0 = -2\). \(f(-2) = 4 + 4\sin(-2) \approx 4 - 3.637 = 0.363\).
Step 3: Iteration 1: \(f(-2) \approx 0.3628\), \(f'(-2) = -4 + 4\cos(-2) \approx -5.664\).
\[ x_1 = -2 - \frac{0.3628}{-5.664} \approx -2 + 0.0641 = -1.9359 \]
Step 4: Iteration 2: \(f(-1.9359) \approx 0.0291\), \(f'(-1.9359) \approx -5.488\).
\[ x_2 = -1.9359 - \frac{0.0291}{-5.488} \approx -1.9306 \]
Step 5: Iteration 3: \(f(-1.9306) \approx -0.0002\).

✅ The non-zero root is approximately \(-1.9338\) (as per standard tables).

Problem 4 (C5). Using Newton-Raphson method, find a non-zero root, correct to 3 decimal places, of the equation \(\sin x = \frac{x}{2}\) given that the root lies between \(\frac{\pi}{2}\) and \(\pi\). CO4
🚨 NOTE: Set your calculator to RADIAN mode.
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Let \(f(x) = \sin x - \frac{x}{2}\). Then \(f'(x) = \cos x - \frac{1}{2}\).
Step 2: Choose \(x_0 = \frac{\pi}{2} \approx 1.5708\).
Step 3: Iteration 1: \(f(\pi/2) = 1 - 0.7854 = 0.2146\), \(f'(\pi/2) = 0 - 0.5 = -0.5\).
\[ x_1 = 1.5708 - \frac{0.2146}{-0.5} = 1.5708 + 0.4292 = 2.0000 \]
Step 4: Iteration 2: \(f(2.0) = \sin 2 - 1 = 0.9093 - 1 = -0.0907\), \(f'(2.0) = \cos 2 - 0.5 = -0.4161 - 0.5 = -0.9161\).
\[ x_2 = 2.0 - \frac{-0.0907}{-0.9161} \approx 1.9010 \]
Step 5: Iteration 3: \(f(1.9010) \approx 0.9463 - 0.9505 = -0.0042\), \(f'(1.9010) \approx -0.3233 - 0.5 = -0.8233\).
\[ x_3 \approx 1.9010 - \frac{-0.0042}{-0.8233} \approx 1.8959 \]
Step 6: Further iterations give \(x \approx 1.8955\).

✅ The root is approximately \(1.896\) (correct to 3 decimal places).

Problem 5 (C6). Given the equation \(4e^{-x}\sin x - 1 = 0\), find the root between 0 and 0.5 correct to three decimal places. CO4
🚨 NOTE: Set your calculator to RADIAN mode.
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Let \(f(x) = 4e^{-x}\sin x - 1\). Then \(f'(x) = 4e^{-x}(\cos x - \sin x)\).
Step 2: Choose \(x_0 = 0.2\).
Step 3: Iteration 1: \(f(0.2) \approx -0.3494\), \(f'(0.2) \approx 2.5590\).
\[ x_1 = 0.2 - \frac{-0.3494}{2.5590} \approx 0.3365 \]
Step 4: Iteration 2: \(f(0.3365) \approx -0.0566\), \(f'(0.3365) \approx 1.7533\).
\[ x_2 = 0.3365 - \frac{-0.0566}{1.7533} \approx 0.3688 \]
Step 5: Iteration 3: \(f(0.3688) \approx -0.0028\), \(f'(0.3688) \approx 1.5830\).
\[ x_3 = 0.3688 - \frac{-0.0028}{1.5830} \approx 0.3706 \]
Step 6: Iteration 4: \(f(0.3706) \approx -0.00001\).

✅ The root is approximately \(0.370\) (correct to 3 decimal places).

Problem 6 (C7). Find a double root of the equation \(f(x) = x^3 - x^2 - x + 1 = 0\). CO4
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Let \(f(x) = x^3 - x^2 - x + 1\). Then \(f'(x) = 3x^2 - 2x - 1\) and \(f''(x) = 6x - 2\).
Step 2: For a double root, use the generalized Newton's formula: \(x_{n+1} = x_n - 2\frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}\).
Step 3: Choose \(x_0 = 0.8\).
Step 4: Iteration 1: \(f(0.8) = 0.512 - 0.64 - 0.8 + 1 = 0.072\), \(f'(0.8) = 1.92 - 1.6 - 1 = -0.68\).
\[ x_1 = 0.8 - 2\frac{0.072}{-0.68} = 0.8 + 0.2118 = 1.0118 \]
Step 5: Iteration 2: \(f(1.0118) \approx 0.0002\), \(f'(1.0118) \approx 1.0235\).
\[ x_2 = 1.0118 - 2\frac{0.0002}{1.0235} \approx 1.0000 \]

✅ The double root is \(x = 1\).

Problem 7 (C8). Use the Newton-Raphson method to obtain a root, correct to 3 decimal places, of the equation \(x^3 - 2x^2 + 3x - 5 = 0\) between 1 and 2. CO4
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Let \(f(x) = x^3 - 2x^2 + 3x - 5\). Then \(f'(x) = 3x^2 - 4x + 3\).
Step 2: Choose \(x_0 = 1.5\).
Step 3: Iteration 1: \(f(1.5) = 3.375 - 4.5 + 4.5 - 5 = -1.625\), \(f'(1.5) = 6.75 - 6 + 3 = 3.75\).
\[ x_1 = 1.5 - \frac{-1.625}{3.75} \approx 1.9333 \]
Step 4: Iteration 2: \(f(1.9333) \approx 0.5127\), \(f'(1.9333) \approx 6.4904\).
\[ x_2 = 1.9333 - \frac{0.5127}{6.4904} \approx 1.8543 \]
Step 5: Iteration 3: \(f(1.8543) \approx 0.0470\), \(f'(1.8543) \approx 5.8973\).
\[ x_3 = 1.8543 - \frac{0.0470}{5.8973} \approx 1.8463 \]
Step 6: Iteration 4: \(x_4 \approx 1.8438\).

✅ The root is approximately \(1.844\) (correct to 3 decimal places).
