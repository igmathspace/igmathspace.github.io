📖 Topic: Canonical Form of LPP – Characteristics & Representation
📚 Textbook: Operations Research – Gupta & Hira | §2.12-1 (Canonical Form)
👨‍🏫 Faculty: Gafoor I 🎯 CO3: Understand canonical form and its transformations ⏱️ Lecture duration: 1 Hour
🎯 1. Learning Outcomes (CO3)
  • State the definition of the canonical form of an LPP
  • List its three defining characteristics
  • Convert any LPP to canonical form using elementary transformations
  • Convert a minimisation problem to canonical (maximisation) form
📌 2. Definition of Canonical Form
Definition

The general LPP can always be put in the following form, called the canonical form:

\[ \begin{aligned} \text{Maximise } Z &= \sum_{j=1}^{n} c_j x_j\\ \text{subject to } & \sum_{j=1}^{n} a_{ij} x_j \le b_i, \quad i = 1, 2, \dots, m,\\ & x_j \ge 0, \quad j = 1, 2, \dots, n. \end{aligned} \]
📐 3. Three Characteristics of the Canonical Form
The three conditions
  1. The objective function is of the maximisation type.
  2. All constraints are of the \((\le)\) type, except the non-negativity restrictions (which are always of the \((\ge)\) type).
  3. All decision variables are non-negative.
🔄 4. Elementary Transformations to Canonical Form
Rule 1 — Convert minimisation to maximisation

Minimisation of a function \(f\) is equivalent to maximisation of \(-f\):

\[ \text{Minimise } Z = c_1 x_1 + \cdots + c_n x_n \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \text{Maximise } G = -Z = -c_1 x_1 - \cdots - c_n x_n, \]

with \(Z = -G\) at the end.

Rule 2 — Change \((\ge)\) to \((\le)\)

Multiply both sides by \(-1\):

\[ a_1 x_1 + a_2 x_2 \ge b \;\Longleftrightarrow\; -a_1 x_1 - a_2 x_2 \le -b. \]
Rule 3 — Replace equations by two opposite inequalities
\[ a_1 x_1 + a_2 x_2 = b \;\Longleftrightarrow\; \begin{cases} a_1 x_1 + a_2 x_2 \le b\\ a_1 x_1 + a_2 x_2 \ge b. \end{cases} \]

(The second can then be converted to \(\le\) via Rule 2.)

Rule 4 — Absolute value constraints

For \(b \ge 0\):

\[ |a_1 x_1 + a_2 x_2| \le b \;\Longleftrightarrow\; -b \le a_1 x_1 + a_2 x_2 \le b, \] \[ |p_1 x_1 + p_2 x_2| \ge q \;\Longleftrightarrow\; p_1 x_1 + p_2 x_2 \ge q \;\text{or}\; p_1 x_1 + p_2 x_2 \le -q. \]
Rule 5 — Unrestricted variables

If a variable is unrestricted in sign, express it as the difference of two non-negative variables:

\[ x = x' - x'', \quad x' \ge 0,\; x'' \ge 0. \]

Value of \(x\) is positive, zero, or negative depending on whether \(x'\) is greater than, equal to, or less than \(x''\).

⚠️ CAUTION! A minimisation problem is also said to be in canonical form if all its variables are non-negative and all its constraints are of \((\ge)\) type. But for the standard canonical form used in the simplex method, we always use the maximisation form.
📚 5. Workout Problems

Problem 1. Express the following LPP in canonical form: Maximise \(Z = 3x_1 + 5x_2 - 2x_3\), subject to \(x_1 + 2x_2 - x_3 \ge -4,\; -5x_1 + 6x_2 + 7x_3 \ge 5,\; 2x_1 + x_2 + 3x_3 = 10,\; x_1, x_2 \ge 0,\; x_3\) unrestricted. CO3
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Objective is already maximisation — Rule 1 not needed.
Step 2: Constraint 1: \(x_1 + 2x_2 - x_3 \ge -4\). By Rule 2, multiply by \(-1\): \(-x_1 - 2x_2 + x_3 \le 4\).
Step 3: Constraint 2: \(-5x_1 + 6x_2 + 7x_3 \ge 5\). Multiply by \(-1\): \(5x_1 - 6x_2 - 7x_3 \le -5\).
Step 4: Constraint 3: \(2x_1 + x_2 + 3x_3 = 10\). By Rule 3, replace by two inequalities:
\(2x_1 + x_2 + 3x_3 \le 10\), and \(2x_1 + x_2 + 3x_3 \ge 10\).
Step 5: Convert the \(\ge\) from step 4 to \(\le\): \(-2x_1 - x_2 - 3x_3 \le -10\).
Step 6: \(x_3\) unrestricted. By Rule 5, let \(x_3 = x_3' - x_3''\) with \(x_3', x_3'' \ge 0\).
Step 7: Substitute \(x_3 = x_3' - x_3''\) in all expressions.
Final canonical form:
\(\begin{aligned}\text{Maximise } Z = &\; 3x_1 + 5x_2 - 2x_3' + 2x_3''\\\text{s.t. } & -x_1 - 2x_2 + x_3' - x_3'' \le 4,\\ & 5x_1 - 6x_2 - 7x_3' + 7x_3'' \le -5,\\ & 2x_1 + x_2 + 3x_3' - 3x_3'' \le 10,\\ & -2x_1 - x_2 - 3x_3' + 3x_3'' \le -10,\\ & x_1, x_2, x_3', x_3'' \ge 0.\end{aligned}\)

✅ Three original constraints became four after eliminating the equality and the unrestricted variable.

Problem 2. Convert to canonical form: Minimise \(Z = 5x_1 + 3x_2\), subject to \(2x_1 + x_2 \ge 6,\; x_1 + 3x_2 \ge 9,\; x_1, x_2 \ge 0\). CO3
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Objective is minimisation. By Rule 1, minimise \(Z = 5x_1 + 3x_2\) is equivalent to maximise \(G = -Z = -5x_1 - 3x_2\).
Step 2: Constraints: \(2x_1 + x_2 \ge 6\) and \(x_1 + 3x_2 \ge 9\). By Rule 2, multiply by \(-1\): \(-2x_1 - x_2 \le -6\) and \(-x_1 - 3x_2 \le -9\).
Step 3: All variables non-negative — OK.
Final canonical form:
\(\begin{aligned}\text{Maximise } G = &\; -5x_1 - 3x_2\\\text{s.t. } & -2x_1 - x_2 \le -6,\\ & -x_1 - 3x_2 \le -9,\\ & x_1, x_2 \ge 0.\end{aligned}\)
Note: \(Z_{\min} = -G_{\max}\) for the original problem.

✅ Objective sign changed; constraints' direction flipped; all variables ≥ 0.

Problem 3. Write in canonical form: Minimise \(Z = 4x_1 + 5x_2 + 6x_3\), subject to \(2x_1 + 3x_3 \ge 6,\; 2x_1 + 3x_2 + 2x_3 = 8,\; 5x_1 - 2x_2 + 5x_3 \le 10,\; x_1, x_2, x_3 \ge 0\). CO3
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Minimisation → maximisation. Let \(G = -Z = -4x_1 - 5x_2 - 6x_3\).
Step 2: Constraint 1: \(2x_1 + 3x_3 \ge 6\) → multiply by \(-1\): \(-2x_1 - 3x_3 \le -6\).
Step 3: Constraint 2: \(2x_1 + 3x_2 + 2x_3 = 8\) → split into two inequalities:
\(2x_1 + 3x_2 + 2x_3 \le 8\), and \(2x_1 + 3x_2 + 2x_3 \ge 8\).
Then flip the \(\ge\) to \(\le\): \(-2x_1 - 3x_2 - 2x_3 \le -8\).
Step 4: Constraint 3 is already \(\le\): \(5x_1 - 2x_2 + 5x_3 \le 10\).
Step 5: All variables non-negative — OK.
Final canonical form:
\(\begin{aligned}\text{Maximise } G = &\; -4x_1 - 5x_2 - 6x_3\\\text{s.t. } & -2x_1 - 3x_3 \le -6,\\ & 2x_1 + 3x_2 + 2x_3 \le 8,\\ & -2x_1 - 3x_2 - 2x_3 \le -8,\\ & 5x_1 - 2x_2 + 5x_3 \le 10,\\ & x_1, x_2, x_3 \ge 0.\end{aligned}\)

✅ All constraints are now of \(\le\) type, all variables non-negative, objective is maximisation.

📋 7. Quick Summary
  • Canonical form: maximisation objective, all constraints \((\le)\), all variables non-negative.
  • Transformations: min → max (change sign), \(\ge\) → \(\le\) (multiply by \(-1\)), equations → two inequalities, absolute value → two inequalities, unrestricted variables → difference of non-negative variables.
  • Uses: canonical form is helpful in dealing with duality theory (§6.1).
