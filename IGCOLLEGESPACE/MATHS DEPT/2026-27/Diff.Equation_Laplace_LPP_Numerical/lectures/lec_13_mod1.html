📖 Orthogonal Trajectories – Method & Solutions
📌 Definition: Orthogonal Trajectories

Orthogonal trajectories are a family of curves that intersect a given family of curves at right angles (90°).

If two curves intersect at a point, the angle between them is defined as the angle between their tangents at that point. Orthogonal is another word for perpendicular.

💡 Key idea: If a curve from the given family has slope \( m \) at a point, the orthogonal trajectory through that point must have slope \( -\frac{1}{m} \) (since the product of slopes of perpendicular lines is \(-1\)).
🔬 Applications of Orthogonal Trajectories
🌡️ Isotherms (equal temperature) & Heat flow curves
🗺️ Contour lines (equal altitude) & Steepest descent curves
⚡ Equipotential curves (equal voltage) & Electric force lines
🧲 Magnetic field lines & Equipotential surfaces
⚡ Method for Finding Orthogonal Trajectories
📐 Step-by-Step Procedure
Step 1: Given a one-parameter family of curves \( G(x, y, c) = 0 \), find an ODE for which this family is the general solution.
  • Differentiate \( G(x, y, c) = 0 \) implicitly with respect to \( x \).
  • Eliminate the parameter \( c \) to obtain an ODE of the form: \[ y' = f(x, y) \]
Step 2: Find the ODE for the orthogonal trajectories.
  • If the given family has slope \( f(x, y) \), the orthogonal trajectories must have slope \( -\frac{1}{f(x, y)} \).
  • Thus, the ODE for the orthogonal trajectories is: \[ \bar{y}' = -\frac{1}{f(x, \bar{y})} \]
  • Equivalently, replace \( y' \) with \( -\frac{1}{y'} \) in the ODE from Step 1.
Step 3: Solve the ODE from Step 2.
  • This ODE is often separable or linear.
  • The general solution \( \bar{y} = \bar{y}(x, c) \) gives the family of orthogonal trajectories.
📝 Practice Problems
1. Find the orthogonal trajectories of \( y^2 = 4cx \)
Step 1: Differentiate \( y^2 = 4cx \) implicitly (differentiate both sides with respect to x)
\( 2y y' = 4c \)
Step 1 (cont): Eliminate \( c \) using \( c = \frac{y^2}{4x} \) (from the original equation)
\( 2y y' = 4 \cdot \frac{y^2}{4x} = \frac{y^2}{x} \) → \( y' = \frac{y}{2x} \) (ODE of the given family)
Step 2: Replace \( y' \) with \( -\frac{1}{y'} \) (negative reciprocal for orthogonal trajectories)
\( \bar{y}' = -\frac{2x}{\bar{y}} \)
Step 3: Separate variables (rearrange to get y terms on one side, x terms on the other)
\( \bar{y} \, d\bar{y} = -2x \, dx \)
Step 3 (cont): Integrate both sides (apply integration)
\( \int \bar{y} \, d\bar{y} = \int -2x \, dx \) → \( \frac{\bar{y}^2}{2} = -x^2 + c \)
Final: Multiply by 2 (simplify to get the family of orthogonal trajectories)
\( \bar{y}^2 + 2x^2 = C \) or \( x^2 + \frac{y^2}{2} = C \) (family of ellipses)
2. Find the orthogonal trajectories of \( x^2 + y^2 = 2cx \)
Step 1: Differentiate \( x^2 + y^2 = 2cx \) implicitly (differentiate both sides with respect to x)
\( 2x + 2y y' = 2c \)
Step 1 (cont): Eliminate \( c \) using \( c = \frac{x^2 + y^2}{2x} \) (from the original equation)
\( 2x + 2y y' = 2 \cdot \frac{x^2 + y^2}{2x} = \frac{x^2 + y^2}{x} \)
\( 2x + 2y y' = x + \frac{y^2}{x} \) → \( 2y y' = \frac{y^2}{x} - x = \frac{y^2 - x^2}{x} \)
\( y' = \frac{y^2 - x^2}{2xy} \) (ODE of the given family)
Step 2: Replace \( y' \) with \( -\frac{1}{y'} \) (negative reciprocal for orthogonal trajectories)
\( \bar{y}' = -\frac{2x\bar{y}}{\bar{y}^2 - x^2} = \frac{2x\bar{y}}{x^2 - \bar{y}^2} \)
Step 3: This is homogeneous. Let \( \bar{y} = vx \) (substitute to solve homogeneous ODE)
\( v + x v' = \frac{2x(vx)}{x^2 - v^2 x^2} = \frac{2v}{1 - v^2} \)
Step 3 (cont): Simplify (rearrange to separate variables)
\( x v' = \frac{2v}{1 - v^2} - v = \frac{2v - v + v^3}{1 - v^2} = \frac{v + v^3}{1 - v^2} = \frac{v(1 + v^2)}{1 - v^2} \)
Step 3 (cont): Separate variables (rearrange to integrate)
\( \frac{1 - v^2}{v(1 + v^2)} dv = \frac{dx}{x} \)
Step 3 (cont): Integrate (apply partial fractions)
\( \ln|v| - \ln|1 + v^2| = \ln|x| + c \) → \( \frac{v}{1 + v^2} = Cx \)
Final: Substitute back \( v = \frac{y}{x} \) (return to original variables)
\( \frac{y/x}{1 + y^2/x^2} = Cx \) → \( \frac{xy}{x^2 + y^2} = Cx \) → \( x^2 + y^2 = \frac{y}{C} \)
\( x^2 + y^2 = ky \) (family of circles passing through the origin)
3. Find the orthogonal trajectories of \( y = cx^2 \)
Step 1: Differentiate \( y = cx^2 \) (differentiate with respect to x)
\( y' = 2cx \)
Step 1 (cont): Eliminate \( c \) using \( c = \frac{y}{x^2} \) (from the original equation)
\( y' = 2 \cdot \frac{y}{x^2} \cdot x = \frac{2y}{x} \) (ODE of the given family)
Step 2: Replace \( y' \) with \( -\frac{1}{y'} \) (negative reciprocal for orthogonal trajectories)
\( \bar{y}' = -\frac{x}{2\bar{y}} \)
Step 3: Separate variables (rearrange to integrate)
\( 2\bar{y} \, d\bar{y} = -x \, dx \)
Step 3 (cont): Integrate both sides (apply integration)
\( \int 2\bar{y} \, d\bar{y} = \int -x \, dx \) → \( \bar{y}^2 = -\frac{x^2}{2} + c \)
Final: Simplify (write in standard form)
\( 2y^2 + x^2 = C \) or \( \frac{x^2}{C} + \frac{y^2}{C/2} = 1 \) (family of ellipses)
4. Find the orthogonal trajectories of \( x^2 + y^2 = c^2 \)
Step 1: Differentiate \( x^2 + y^2 = c^2 \) implicitly (differentiate both sides with respect to x)
\( 2x + 2y y' = 0 \)
Step 1 (cont): Solve for \( y' \) (rearrange to get ODE)
\( y' = -\frac{x}{y} \) (ODE of the given family — circles centered at origin)
Step 2: Replace \( y' \) with \( -\frac{1}{y'} \) (negative reciprocal for orthogonal trajectories)
\( \bar{y}' = \frac{\bar{y}}{x} \)
Step 3: Separate variables (rearrange to integrate)
\( \frac{d\bar{y}}{\bar{y}} = \frac{dx}{x} \)
Step 3 (cont): Integrate both sides (apply integration)
\( \ln|\bar{y}| = \ln|x| + c \)
Final: Take exponents (solve for y)
\( \bar{y} = kx \) (family of straight lines through the origin — radial lines)
5. Find the orthogonal trajectories of \( y = c e^{x} \)
Step 1: Differentiate \( y = c e^{x} \) (differentiate with respect to x)
\( y' = c e^{x} \)
Step 1 (cont): Eliminate \( c \) using \( c = y e^{-x} \) (from the original equation)
\( y' = y e^{-x} \cdot e^{x} = y \) (ODE of the given family)
Step 2: Replace \( y' \) with \( -\frac{1}{y'} \) (negative reciprocal for orthogonal trajectories)
\( \bar{y}' = -\frac{1}{\bar{y}} \)
Step 3: Separate variables (rearrange to integrate)
\( \bar{y} \, d\bar{y} = -dx \)
Step 3 (cont): Integrate both sides (apply integration)
\( \int \bar{y} \, d\bar{y} = \int -dx \) → \( \frac{\bar{y}^2}{2} = -x + c \)
Final: Multiply by 2 (write in standard form)
\( y^2 = C - 2x \) or \( y^2 + 2x = C \) (family of parabolas)
📝 Important Notes
  • Angle of intersection: The angle between two curves is the angle between their tangents at the intersection point.
  • Orthogonal condition: Two curves are orthogonal if the product of their slopes at the intersection point is \(-1\): \[ m_1 \cdot m_2 = -1 \]
  • Special case: If the given family has vertical tangents (\( f(x,y) = \infty \)), the orthogonal trajectories have horizontal tangents, and vice versa.
  • One-parameter family: The method applies to families of curves that depend on a single parameter \( c \).
📋 Quick Summary

Given family: \( G(x, y, c) = 0 \)

Step 1: Differentiate → eliminate \( c \) → \( y' = f(x, y) \)
Step 2: Replace \( y' \) with \( -\frac{1}{y'} \) → \( \bar{y}' = -\frac{1}{f(x, \bar{y})} \)
Step 3: Solve the ODE → orthogonal trajectories \( \bar{y}(x, c) \)
🥇 Key Takeaway: Orthogonal trajectories are found by replacing the slope \( y' \) with its negative reciprocal \( -1/y' \) in the ODE of the given family.
