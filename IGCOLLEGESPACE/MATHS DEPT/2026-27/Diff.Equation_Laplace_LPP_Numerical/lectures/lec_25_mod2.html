📌 Module II | Laplace Transforms
📖 Topic: Inverse Laplace – Complex Conjugate Roots & Completing the Square
📚 Textbook: Advanced Engineering Mathematics – E. Kreyszig | Sections 6.1–6.2 (Table 6.1)
👨‍🏫 Faculty: Gafoor I 🎯 CO2: Understand Laplace Transform and inverse transforms ⏱️ Lecture duration: 1 Hour
🎯 1. Learning Outcomes (CO2)
  • Recognise irreducible quadratic factors and complete the square
  • Use the shifted forms \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{s-a}{(s-a)^2+\omega^2}\right\} = e^{at}\cos\omega t \) and \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{\omega}{(s-a)^2+\omega^2}\right\} = e^{at}\sin\omega t \)
  • Split the numerator to match shifted forms
  • Handle repeated quadratic factors at a basic level (completing the square + standard table)
📊 2. Key Standard Forms (Shifted)
\[ \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{s-a}{(s-a)^2+\omega^2}\right\} = e^{at}\cos\omega t \] \[ \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{\omega}{(s-a)^2+\omega^2}\right\} = e^{at}\sin\omega t \] \[ \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{s}{(s)^2+\omega^2}\right\} = \cos\omega t, \quad \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\frac{1}{s^2+\omega^2}\right\} = \frac{\sin\omega t}{\omega} \]
📐 3. Completing the Square – The Core Technique

For a denominator \( s^2 + bs + c \) with \( b^2 - 4c < 0 \):

\[ s^2 + bs + c = \left(s + \tfrac{b}{2}\right)^2 + \left(c - \tfrac{b^2}{4}\right) \]

Write \( a = -\tfrac{b}{2} \) and \( \omega = \sqrt{c - \tfrac{b^2}{4}} \), so the denominator becomes \( (s-a)^2 + \omega^2 \). Then adjust the numerator to match the shifted forms.

⚠️ CAUTION! The numerator must be rewritten as \( \alpha(s-a) + \beta \) to split into a cosine term and a sine term. Never leave \( s \) unshifted in the numerator when the denominator is shifted.

Problem 1. If \( \mathcal{L}\{f(t)\} = \dfrac{3s-137}{s^2+2s+401} \), find \( f(t) \). CO2
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Complete the square: \( s^2+2s+401 = (s+1)^2 + 400 = (s+1)^2 + 20^2 \).
Step 2: Here \( a = -1 \), so the shifted variable is \( (s+1) \), and \( \omega = 20 \).
Step 3: Rewrite the numerator in terms of \( (s+1) \): \( 3s - 137 = 3(s+1) - 140 \).
Step 4: Split: \( \dfrac{3(s+1) - 140}{(s+1)^2+20^2} = 3\cdot\dfrac{s+1}{(s+1)^2+20^2} - \dfrac{140}{(s+1)^2+20^2} \).
Step 5: Match the sine form: \( \dfrac{140}{(s+1)^2+20^2} = \dfrac{140}{20}\cdot\dfrac{20}{(s+1)^2+20^2} = 7\cdot\dfrac{20}{(s+1)^2+20^2} \).
Step 6: Invert the first term: \( 3\cdot e^{-t}\cos 20t \).
Step 7: Invert the second term: \( 7\cdot e^{-t}\sin 20t \).
Step 8: Combine: \( f(t) = 3e^{-t}\cos 20t - 7e^{-t}\sin 20t \).

✅ \( f(t) = e^{-t}(3\cos 20t - 7\sin 20t) \).

Problem 2. Find \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{6s+7}{2s^2+4s+10}\right\} \). CO2
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Factor out 2 from the denominator: \( 2s^2+4s+10 = 2(s^2+2s+5) \).
Step 2: Complete the square: \( s^2+2s+5 = (s+1)^2 + 4 = (s+1)^2 + 2^2 \). Here \( a = -1 \), \( \omega = 2 \).
Step 3: Write \( \dfrac{6s+7}{2s^2+4s+10} = \dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{6s+7}{(s+1)^2+4} \).
Step 4: Rewrite numerator: \( 6s+7 = 6(s+1) + 1 \).
Step 5: Split: \( \dfrac{6(s+1)+1}{(s+1)^2+4} = 6\cdot\dfrac{s+1}{(s+1)^2+4} + \dfrac{1}{(s+1)^2+4} \).
Step 6: For the sine term: \( \dfrac{1}{(s+1)^2+4} = \dfrac{1}{2}\cdot\dfrac{2}{(s+1)^2+4} \).
Step 7: Invert with the \( \tfrac{1}{2} \) overall factor: \( \tfrac{1}{2}\left[6 e^{-t}\cos 2t + \tfrac{1}{2} e^{-t}\sin 2t\right] \).
Step 8: Simplify: \( 3 e^{-t}\cos 2t + \tfrac{1}{4} e^{-t}\sin 2t \).

✅ \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{6s+7}{2s^2+4s+10}\right\} = e^{-t}\left(3\cos 2t + \tfrac{1}{4}\sin 2t\right) \).

Problem 3. Find \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s(s^2+\omega^2)}\right\} \) and \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s^2(s^2+\omega^2)}\right\} \). CO2
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Standard: \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s^2+\omega^2}\right\} = \dfrac{\sin\omega t}{\omega} \).
Step 2: Use the integral theorem \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s}F(s)\right\} = \displaystyle\int_0^t f(\tau)d\tau \).
Step 3: \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s(s^2+\omega^2)}\right\} = \displaystyle\int_0^t \dfrac{\sin\omega\tau}{\omega}d\tau \).
Step 4: Evaluate: \( = \dfrac{1}{\omega}\left[-\dfrac{\cos\omega\tau}{\omega}\right]_0^t = \dfrac{1-\cos\omega t}{\omega^2} \).
Step 5: Apply the integral theorem again: \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s^2(s^2+\omega^2)}\right\} = \displaystyle\int_0^t \dfrac{1-\cos\omega\tau}{\omega^2}d\tau \).
Step 6: Evaluate: \( = \dfrac{1}{\omega^2}\left[\tau - \dfrac{\sin\omega\tau}{\omega}\right]_0^t = \dfrac{t}{\omega^2} - \dfrac{\sin\omega t}{\omega^3} \).

✅ \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s(s^2+\omega^2)}\right\} = \dfrac{1-\cos\omega t}{\omega^2} \), \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s^2(s^2+\omega^2)}\right\} = \dfrac{t}{\omega^2} - \dfrac{\sin\omega t}{\omega^3} \).

Problem 4. Find \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{a}{(s+1)^2}\right\} \). CO2
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Standard shift formula: \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{(s-a)^n}\right\} = \dfrac{t^{n-1}e^{at}}{(n-1)!} \).
Step 2: Here \( n=2 \), \( a = -1 \), so \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{(s+1)^2}\right\} = \dfrac{t^{1}e^{-t}}{1!} = t e^{-t} \).
Step 3: By linearity: \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{a}{(s+1)^2}\right\} = a\,t e^{-t} \).

✅ \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{a}{(s+1)^2}\right\} = a t e^{-t} \).

📋 6. Quick Summary
  • Complete the square: \( s^2+bs+c = (s+\tfrac{b}{2})^2 + (c-\tfrac{b^2}{4}) \).
  • Shifted forms: \( \dfrac{s-a}{(s-a)^2+\omega^2} \to e^{at}\cos\omega t \), \( \dfrac{\omega}{(s-a)^2+\omega^2} \to e^{at}\sin\omega t \).
  • Split numerator: write \( \alpha(s-a)+\beta \) to match both forms.
  • Often combined: with the integral theorem for factors like \( 1/s \) in front.
