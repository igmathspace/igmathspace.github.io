📐 2. Multiple Roots and the Generalized Newton's Method
📌 Problem with Multiple Roots

If \(\xi\) is a root of \(f(x) = 0\) with multiplicity \(p > 1\), then \(f(\xi) = f'(\xi) = \dots = f^{(p-1)}(\xi) = 0\). The standard Newton-Raphson formula

\[ x_{n+1} = x_n - \frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)} \]

still converges, but only linearly (instead of quadratically), because \(f'(\xi) = 0\) makes the error recurrence degenerate.

📌 Generalized Newton's Formula

If \(\xi\) is a root of multiplicity \(p\), the modified iteration is:

\[ \boxed{x_{n+1} = x_n - p\,\frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}} \]

This restores quadratic convergence. For \(p = 1\), it reduces to the standard formula.

How to estimate \(p\): Near a multiple root, the quantity

\[ p \approx \frac{x_{n+1} - x_n}{x_{n+2} - x_{n+1}} \quad \text{or} \quad p \approx \frac{f(x_n)/f'(x_n)}{\text{correction term}} \]

In practice, one computes \(\delta_n = f(x_n)/f'(x_n)\) and observes that \(\delta_n\) stabilizes to a constant ratio related to \(1/p\).

⚠️ 3. Pitfalls and Conditions for Failure
🚨 Common Pitfalls
  • Zero derivative: If \(f'(x_n) = 0\), the method fails (division by zero).
  • Near-zero derivative: If \(f'(x_n)\) is very small, the correction term is huge and the iteration may overshoot.
  • Poor initial guess: Newton-Raphson is only locally convergent; a bad \(x_0\) may diverge or oscillate.
  • Repeated roots: Standard formula slows to linear convergence; use the generalized formula.
  • Inflection points: If \(f''(x_n) = 0\) near the root, convergence may be slower or erratic.
  • Trigonometric mode: Always use RADIAN mode for trigonometric functions.

Problem 1 (C7). Find a double root of \(x^3 - x^2 - x + 1 = 0\). CO4
✅ Solution:
Let \(f(x) = x^3 - x^2 - x + 1\); then \(f'(x) = 3x^2 - 2x - 1\).
Since \(f(1) = 0\), \(f'(1) = 0\), and \(f''(1) = 6 \neq 0\), \(x = 1\) is a double root (\(p = 2\)).
Apply the generalized Newton formula: \(x_{n+1} = x_n - 2\frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}\).
Choose \(x_0 = 0.8\).
Iteration 1: \(f(0.8) = 0.072\), \(f'(0.8) = -0.68\).
\[ x_1 = 0.8 - 2\frac{0.072}{-0.68} \approx 1.0118 \]
Iteration 2: \(f(1.0118) \approx 0.0002\), \(f'(1.0118) \approx 1.0235\).
\[ x_2 = 1.0118 - 2\frac{0.0002}{1.0235} \approx 1.0000 \]

✅ Double root \(x = 1\).

Problem 2 (C4). Find a non-zero root of \(x^2 + 4\sin x = 0\). CO4
🚨 Use RADIAN mode for trigonometric evaluations.
✅ Solution:
Let \(f(x) = x^2 + 4\sin x\); then \(f'(x) = 2x + 4\cos x\).
Note: \(f'(0) = 4 \neq 0\), so \(x = 0\) is a simple root (but we seek a non-zero root).
Choose \(x_0 = -2\).
Iteration 1: \(f(-2) \approx 0.3628\), \(f'(-2) \approx -5.664\).
\[ x_1 = -2 - \frac{0.3628}{-5.664} \approx -1.9359 \]
Iteration 2: \(f(-1.9359) \approx 0.0291\), \(f'(-1.9359) \approx -5.488\).
\[ x_2 \approx -1.9306 \]
Iteration 3: \(f(-1.9306) \approx -0.0002\), so the root is \(x \approx -1.9338\).

✅ Non-zero root ≈ \(-1.9338\).

Problem 3 (C9). Compute, to four decimal places, the root between 1 and 2 of \(x^3 - 2x^2 + 3x - 5 = 0\) by Newton-Raphson. CO4
✅ Solution:
Let \(f(x) = x^3 - 2x^2 + 3x - 5\); then \(f'(x) = 3x^2 - 4x + 3\).
Choose \(x_0 = 1.5\).
Iteration 1: \(f(1.5) = -1.625\), \(f'(1.5) = 3.75\).
\[ x_1 = 1.5 - \frac{-1.625}{3.75} \approx 1.9333 \]
Iteration 2: \(f(1.9333) \approx 0.5127\), \(f'(1.9333) \approx 6.4904\).
\[ x_2 \approx 1.8543 \]
Iteration 3: \(f(1.8543) \approx 0.0470\), \(f'(1.8543) \approx 5.8973\).
\[ x_3 \approx 1.8463 \]
Iteration 4: \(f(1.8463) \approx 0.0020\), \(f'(1.8463) \approx 5.8109\).
\[ x_4 \approx 1.8438 \]
Iteration 5: \(x_5 \approx 1.8437\).

✅ Root ≈ \(1.8437\) (correct to 4 decimal places).

Problem 4 (A4). Write the Generalized Newton's formula to find an approximate root of \(f(x) = 0\). CO4
✅ Solution:
If \(\xi\) is a root of \(f(x) = 0\) with multiplicity \(p\), the generalized Newton's formula is:
\[ \boxed{x_{n+1} = x_n - p\,\frac{f(x_n)}{f'(x_n)}} \]
For \(p = 1\) (simple root), it reduces to the standard Newton-Raphson formula \(x_{n+1} = x_n - f(x_n)/f'(x_n)\).
The multiplicity \(p\) can be estimated using the fact that \(\xi\) is also a root of \(f'(x) = 0\) with multiplicity \(p-1\).
