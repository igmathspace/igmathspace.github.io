📖 Linear ODEs – Applications & Population Dynamics
📚 Kreyszig §1.5 (Linear ODEs) – Newton's Law of Cooling, Air Circulation, Population Dynamics
📌 Definition of Linear ODE

A first-order ODE is said to be linear if it can be brought into the form:

\[ y' + p(x)y = r(x) \]

and nonlinear if it cannot be brought into this form.

Key Features:
  • The equation is linear in both the unknown function \(y\) and its derivative \(y' = dy/dx\)
  • \(p(x)\) and \(r(x)\) may be any given functions of \(x\)
  • If the independent variable is time, we write \(t\) instead of \(x\)
📐 Standard Form: If the first term is \(f(x)y'\) (instead of \(y'\)), divide the equation by \(f(x)\) to get the standard form with \(y'\) as the first term.
🚀 Shortcut Method for Solving Linear ODEs

📋 Step-by-Step Shortcut

1 Rewrite the Equation:
Rewrite the equation in the form: \[ y' + p(x)y = q(x) \] and identify \(p(x)\) and \(q(x)\) with their correct signs.
2 Find the Integrating Factor:
\[ \boxed{\text{I.F} = e^{\int p(x)\,dx}} \]
3 Finalize the Solution:
Use the ready-made solution frame:
\[ \boxed{y \times \text{I.F} = \int (q(x) \times \text{I.F})\,dx} \]
📝 Note: Here \(y\) is the dependent variable and \(x\) is the independent variable. As per the variable names, the solution frame has to change accordingly.
🌡️ Newton's Law of Cooling

PROBLEM Newton's Law of Cooling

Problem: If the temperature of a cake is 300°F when it leaves the oven and is 200°F ten minutes later, when will it be practically equal to the room temperature of 60°F, say, when will it be 61°F?

📝 Step-by-Step Solution:

1 Set up the Model:
Newton's Law of Cooling: \(\frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - T_s)\), where \(T_s\) is the surrounding temperature.
Here \(T_s = 60°F\).
\[ \frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - 60) \] Standard form: \[ \frac{dT}{dt} + kT = 60k \] So \(p(t) = k\), \(q(t) = 60k\).
2 Find the Integrating Factor:
\[ \text{I.F} = e^{\int k\,dt} = e^{kt} \]
3 Apply the Solution Frame:
\[ T e^{kt} = \int 60k e^{kt}\,dt = 60 e^{kt} + C \] \[ T = 60 + Ce^{-kt} \]
4 Apply Initial Condition \(T(0) = 300\):
\[ 300 = 60 + C \Rightarrow C = 240 \] \[ T(t) = 60 + 240e^{-kt} \]
5 Find \(k\) using \(T(10) = 200\):
\[ 200 = 60 + 240e^{-10k} \] \[ 140 = 240e^{-10k} \Rightarrow e^{-10k} = \frac{140}{240} = \frac{7}{12} \] \[ -10k = \ln\left(\frac{7}{12}\right) \Rightarrow k = -\frac{1}{10}\ln\left(\frac{7}{12}\right) = \frac{1}{10}\ln\left(\frac{12}{7}\right) \]
6 Find time when \(T = 61°F\):
\[ 61 = 60 + 240e^{-kt} \] \[ 1 = 240e^{-kt} \Rightarrow e^{-kt} = \frac{1}{240} \] \[ -kt = \ln\left(\frac{1}{240}\right) = -\ln(240) \] \[ t = \frac{\ln(240)}{k} = \frac{\ln(240)}{\frac{1}{10}\ln(12/7)} = \frac{10\ln(240)}{\ln(12/7)} \] \[ t \approx \frac{10(5.4806)}{0.5390} \approx 101.7 \text{ minutes} \]
7 Final Answer:
\[ \boxed{t \approx 101.7 \text{ minutes} \approx 1 \text{ hour and } 42 \text{ minutes}} \]
💨 Air Circulation Problem

PROBLEM Air Circulation in a Room

Problem: In a room containing 20,000 ft³ of air, 600 ft³ of fresh air flows in per minute, and the mixture (made practically uniform by circulating fans) is exhausted at a rate of 600 cubic feet per minute (cfm). What is the amount of fresh air \(y(t)\) at any time if \(y(0) = 0\)? After what time will 90% of the air be fresh?

📝 Step-by-Step Solution:

1 Set up the Model:
The rate of change of fresh air = Rate in - Rate out.
Rate in = 600 ft³/min (fresh air coming in).
Rate out = (600/20000) × y(t) = 0.03y(t) (mixture exhausted).
\[ \frac{dy}{dt} = 600 - 0.03y \] Standard form: \[ \frac{dy}{dt} + 0.03y = 600 \] So \(p(t) = 0.03\), \(q(t) = 600\).
2 Find the Integrating Factor:
\[ \text{I.F} = e^{\int 0.03\,dt} = e^{0.03t} \]
3 Apply the Solution Frame:
\[ y e^{0.03t} = \int 600 e^{0.03t}\,dt = \frac{600}{0.03} e^{0.03t} + C = 20000 e^{0.03t} + C \] \[ y = 20000 + Ce^{-0.03t} \]
4 Apply Initial Condition \(y(0) = 0\):
\[ 0 = 20000 + C \Rightarrow C = -20000 \] \[ y(t) = 20000(1 - e^{-0.03t}) \]
5 Find time when 90% of air is fresh:
90% of 20000 = 18000 ft³.
\[ 18000 = 20000(1 - e^{-0.03t}) \] \[ 0.9 = 1 - e^{-0.03t} \Rightarrow e^{-0.03t} = 0.1 \] \[ -0.03t = \ln(0.1) \Rightarrow t = \frac{-\ln(0.1)}{0.03} = \frac{\ln(10)}{0.03} \] \[ t \approx \frac{2.3026}{0.03} \approx 76.75 \text{ minutes} \]
6 Final Answer:
Amount of fresh air at time \(t\): \[ \boxed{y(t) = 20000(1 - e^{-0.03t}) \text{ ft}^3} \] Time for 90% fresh air: \[ \boxed{t \approx 76.75 \text{ minutes} \approx 1 \text{ hour and } 17 \text{ minutes}} \]
👥 Population Dynamics
Malthus's Law (Exponential Growth): \[ y' = Ay \Rightarrow y = y_0 e^{At} \] This models populations with unlimited resources.
Logistic Equation (Verhulst Equation): \[ y' = Ay - By^2 = Ay\left(1 - \frac{B}{A}y\right) \]
Solution: \[ y = \frac{A}{B + Ce^{-At}} \]
Carrying Capacity (Limit): \[ \lim_{t\to\infty} y = \frac{A}{B} \]

EXAMPLE Logistic Population Model

Problem: Solve the logistic equation \(y' = Ay - By^2\) with initial condition \(y(0) = y_0\).

📝 Step-by-Step Solution:

1 Rewrite the Equation:
\[ y' = Ay - By^2 \] This is a Bernoulli equation. Let \(u = 1/y\). Then \(y = 1/u\) and \(y' = -u'/u^2\).
Substituting: \[ -\frac{u'}{u^2} = A\frac{1}{u} - B\frac{1}{u^2} \] Multiply by \(-u^2\): \[ u' = -Au + B \] Standard form: \[ u' + Au = B \] So \(p(t) = A\), \(q(t) = B\).
2 Find the Integrating Factor:
\[ \text{I.F} = e^{\int A\,dt} = e^{At} \]
3 Apply the Solution Frame:
\[ u e^{At} = \int B e^{At}\,dt = \frac{B}{A} e^{At} + C \] \[ u = \frac{B}{A} + Ce^{-At} \]
4 Substitute back \(u = 1/y\):
\[ \frac{1}{y} = \frac{B}{A} + Ce^{-At} \] \[ y = \frac{1}{\frac{B}{A} + Ce^{-At}} = \frac{A}{B + ACe^{-At}} \]
5 Apply Initial Condition \(y(0) = y_0\):
\[ y_0 = \frac{A}{B + AC} \Rightarrow B + AC = \frac{A}{y_0} \Rightarrow C = \frac{1}{A}\left(\frac{A}{y_0} - B\right) = \frac{1}{y_0} - \frac{B}{A} \] \[ y = \frac{A}{B + A\left(\frac{1}{y_0} - \frac{B}{A}\right)e^{-At}} = \frac{A}{B + \left(\frac{A}{y_0} - B\right)e^{-At}} \]
6 Final Answer:
\[ \boxed{y(t) = \frac{A}{B + \left(\frac{A}{y_0} - B\right)e^{-At}}} \]
Carrying Capacity: \[ \boxed{\lim_{t\to\infty} y(t) = \frac{A}{B}} \]
⚖️ Stable & Unstable Equilibrium Solutions
Autonomous ODE: \(y' = f(y)\) (independent variable \(t\) does not appear explicitly).
Equilibrium Solutions (Critical Points): Solutions where \(y' = 0\), i.e., \(f(y) = 0\). These are constant solutions.
Stability Classification:
  • Stable: Solutions close to the equilibrium remain close for all future time.
  • Unstable: Solutions initially close to the equilibrium do not remain close as \(t\) increases.

EXAMPLE Logistic Equation Equilibrium

For the logistic equation \(y' = Ay - By^2\):

  • Critical points: \(y = 0\) and \(y = A/B\)
  • \(y = 0\) is unstable (solutions move away from 0)
  • \(y = A/B\) is stable (solutions approach this value)
Phase Line Plot for \(y' = (y-1)(y-2)\):
  • \(y_1 = 1\) is stable
  • \(y_2 = 2\) is unstable
📋 Summary of Applications
Application ODE Model Solution Key Result
Newton's Law of Cooling \(\frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - T_s)\) \(T = T_s + (T_0 - T_s)e^{-kt}\) Finds cooling time
Air Circulation \(\frac{dy}{dt} = \text{In} - \text{Out}\) \(y = C(1 - e^{-rt/V})\) Time to reach % freshness
Malthusian Growth \(y' = Ay\) \(y = y_0 e^{At}\) Exponential growth
Logistic Growth \(y' = Ay - By^2\) \(y = \frac{A}{B + Ce^{-At}}\) Carrying capacity \(A/B\)
⚠️ Common Mistakes to Avoid
MistakeCorrection
Not identifying the surrounding temperature in cooling problems Newton's Law: \(\frac{dT}{dt} = -k(T - T_s)\)
Forgetting that the mixture is uniform in circulation problems Rate out = (outflow rate / total volume) × current amount
Confusing stable and unstable equilibrium Stable: solutions approach; Unstable: solutions move away
Omitting the integration constant \(C\) Always include \(C\) and apply initial conditions
