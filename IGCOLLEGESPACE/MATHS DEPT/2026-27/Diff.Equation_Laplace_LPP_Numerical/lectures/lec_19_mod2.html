📖 Topic: Transforms of Derivatives (First & Second Order)
📚 Textbook: Advanced Engineering Mathematics – E. Kreyszig | Section 6.2
👨‍🏫 Faculty: Gafoor I 🎯 CO2: Understand Laplace Transform, transforms of derivatives ⏱️ Lecture duration: 1 Hour
🎯 1. Learning Outcomes (CO2)
  • State the derivative theorem for the first derivative \( \mathcal{L}\{f'(t)\} \)
  • State the derivative theorem for the second derivative \( \mathcal{L}\{f''(t)\} \)
  • Prove the first-derivative theorem using integration by parts
  • Apply the theorems to compute transforms such as \( \mathcal{L}\{t\sin\omega t\} \), \( \mathcal{L}\{\cos\omega t\} \), \( \mathcal{L}\{\sin\omega t\} \)
📌 2. Recall: Definition of the Laplace Transform

For a function \( f(t) \) defined for \( t \ge 0 \),

\[ \mathcal{L}\{f(t)\} = F(s) = \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-st} f(t)\, dt \]

provided the integral converges (for \( s > k \)).

📐 3. Theorem 1: Transform of the First Derivative
Derivative Theorem (First Order)

Suppose \( f(t) \) is continuous for all \( t \ge 0 \) and satisfies a growth restriction \( |f(t)| \le M e^{kt} \). Suppose \( f'(t) \) is piecewise continuous on every finite interval of the semi-axis \( t \ge 0 \). Then

\[ \boxed{\; \mathcal{L}\{f'(t)\} = s\,\mathcal{L}\{f(t)\} - f(0) \; } \]

Equivalently, with \( F(s) = \mathcal{L}\{f(t)\} \), \( \mathcal{L}\{f'\} = sF(s) - f(0) \).

📝 4. Proof of Theorem 1

By definition and integration by parts:

\( \displaystyle \mathcal{L}\{f'(t)\} = \int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-st} f'(t)\, dt \)

Take \( u = e^{-st} \), \( dv = f'(t)\,dt \). Then \( du = -s e^{-st} dt \), \( v = f(t) \):

\( \displaystyle = \Big[ e^{-st} f(t) \Big]_{0}^{\infty} + s\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-st} f(t)\, dt \)

At the upper limit: since \( |f(t)| \le M e^{kt} \), for \( s > k \), \( e^{-st} f(t) \to 0 \) as \( t \to \infty \).

At the lower limit \( t = 0 \): \( e^{0} f(0) = f(0) \), contributing \( -f(0) \).

\( \displaystyle = 0 - f(0) + s\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-st} f(t)\, dt = sF(s) - f(0). \)

✅ This proves \( \mathcal{L}\{f'(t)\} = sF(s) - f(0) \).

📐 5. Theorem 2: Transform of the Second Derivative
Derivative Theorem (Second Order)

Suppose \( f(t) \) and \( f'(t) \) are continuous for all \( t \ge 0 \) and satisfy the growth restriction, and \( f''(t) \) is piecewise continuous on every finite interval of \( t \ge 0 \). Then

\[ \boxed{\; \mathcal{L}\{f''(t)\} = s^{2}\,\mathcal{L}\{f(t)\} - s\,f(0) - f'(0) \; } \]

Proof. Apply Theorem 1 to \( g(t) = f'(t) \):

\( \displaystyle \mathcal{L}\{f''(t)\} = \mathcal{L}\{g'(t)\} = s\,\mathcal{L}\{g(t)\} - g(0) \)
\( \displaystyle = s\,\mathcal{L}\{f'(t)\} - f'(0) \)
\( \displaystyle = s\big[ s\,\mathcal{L}\{f(t)\} - f(0) \big] - f'(0) \)
\( \displaystyle = s^{2}\,\mathcal{L}\{f(t)\} - s\,f(0) - f'(0). \qquad \blacksquare \)
⚠️ CAUTION! The derivative theorem is a differentiation-to-algebra conversion: differentiation of \( f \) becomes multiplication of \( F(s) \) by \( s \), minus the initial values. Do not forget the initial-value terms!
📊 6. Important Consequences of the Derivative Theorem

Re-derivation of standard transforms:

\( \cos\omega t \): with \( f = \cos\omega t \), \( f(0)=1 \), \( f'' = -\omega^{2}\cos\omega t \), so \( -\omega^2 F = s^2F - s \), giving \( F = \dfrac{s}{s^2+\omega^2} \).
\( \sin\omega t \): with \( g = \sin\omega t \), \( g(0)=0 \), \( g' = \omega\cos\omega t \), so \( s\,\mathcal{L}\{\sin\omega t\} = \omega\,\mathcal{L}\{\cos\omega t\} \), giving \( \mathcal{L}\{\sin\omega t\} = \dfrac{\omega}{s^2+\omega^2} \).
\( t\sin\omega t \): with \( f = t\sin\omega t \), \( f(0)=0 \), \( f'(0)=0 \), \( f'' = 2\omega\cos\omega t - \omega^{2}t\sin\omega t \), so \( 2\omega\cdot\dfrac{s}{s^2+\omega^2} - \omega^{2}F = s^{2}F \), giving \( \mathcal{L}\{t\sin\omega t\} = \dfrac{2\omega s}{(s^{2}+\omega^{2})^{2}} \).
📚 7. Workout Problem

Click Answer to see the step-by-step solution.

Workout Problem. If \( \mathcal{L}\{f(t)\} = F(s) \), find the Laplace transform of \( f'(t) \). Hence state \( \mathcal{L}\{f''(t)\} \). CO2
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: By definition, \( \mathcal{L}\{f'(t)\} = \displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-st} f'(t)\, dt \).
Step 2: Integrate by parts with \( u = e^{-st} \), \( dv = f'(t)\,dt \).
Step 3: Then \( du = -s e^{-st} dt \), \( v = f(t) \).
Step 4: \( \mathcal{L}\{f'(t)\} = \Big[ e^{-st} f(t) \Big]_{0}^{\infty} + s\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty} e^{-st} f(t)\, dt \).
Step 5: Using the growth restriction, \( e^{-st} f(t) \to 0 \) as \( t \to \infty \) for \( s > k \).
Step 6: At \( t = 0 \) the bracket contributes \( -f(0) \).
Step 7: Hence \( \mathcal{L}\{f'(t)\} = s F(s) - f(0) \).
Step 8: Apply the first-derivative formula to \( f' \) to get \( \mathcal{L}\{f''(t)\} = s\,\mathcal{L}\{f'(t)\} - f'(0) \).
Step 9: Substitute Step 7: \( \mathcal{L}\{f''(t)\} = s[sF(s) - f(0)] - f'(0) \).
Step 10: Therefore \( \mathcal{L}\{f''(t)\} = s^{2}F(s) - s\,f(0) - f'(0) \).

✅ First derivative: \( sF(s) - f(0) \); second derivative: \( s^2F(s) - s f(0) - f'(0) \).

🏠 8. Homework (Minimum 5 Problems)
  1. State the derivative theorem for the first derivative and write down the formula for the second derivative. (3 marks)
  2. Using \( \mathcal{L}\{\cos\omega t\} = \dfrac{s}{s^2+\omega^2} \) and the derivative theorem, derive \( \mathcal{L}\{\sin\omega t\} = \dfrac{\omega}{s^2+\omega^2} \). (6 marks)
  3. Let \( f(t) = t\sin\omega t \). Verify that \( f(0) = 0 \), \( f'(0) = 0 \), \( f''(t) = 2\omega\cos\omega t - \omega^2 t\sin\omega t \), and hence obtain \( \mathcal{L}\{t\sin\omega t\} = \dfrac{2\omega s}{(s^2+\omega^2)^2} \). (6 marks)
  4. If \( \mathcal{L}\{f(t)\} = \dfrac{1}{s^2+1} \), find \( \mathcal{L}\{f'(t)\} \) and \( \mathcal{L}\{f''(t)\} \) using the derivative theorems. (6 marks)
  5. Using the derivative theorem, find \( \mathcal{L}\{t\cos\omega t\} \). (Hint: take \( f = t\cos\omega t \) and compute \( f'' \).) (6 marks)
  6. State the conditions under which the first-derivative theorem holds, and explain why the growth restriction is needed. (6 marks)
📋 9. Quick Summary
  • First derivative: \( \mathcal{L}\{f'\} = sF(s) - f(0) \)
  • Second derivative: \( \mathcal{L}\{f''\} = s^{2}F(s) - s f(0) - f'(0) \)
  • Key idea: differentiation in \( t \) ↔ multiplication by \( s \) in \( s \), minus initial values.
  • Technique: integration by parts is the engine of the proof.
