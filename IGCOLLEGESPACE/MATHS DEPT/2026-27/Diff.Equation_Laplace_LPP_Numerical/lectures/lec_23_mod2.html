🎯 1. Learning Outcomes (CO2)
  • Define the inverse Laplace transform \( \mathcal{L}^{-1} \)
  • State and use the linearity property of \( \mathcal{L}^{-1} \)
  • Recall the standard inverse pairs (Table 6.1)
  • Apply basic partial-fraction splitting and identify inverse transforms of simple rational functions
📌 2. Definition: Inverse Laplace Transform
Definition

If \( \mathcal{L}\{f(t)\} = F(s) \), then \( f(t) \) is called the inverse Laplace transform of \( F(s) \) and we write

\[ \boxed{\; f(t) = \mathcal{L}^{-1}\{F(s)\} \; } \]

The inverse is defined for a given \( F(s) \) (and unique in the class of continuous functions).

📐 3. Theorem: Linearity of the Inverse Transform
Linearity Property

For constants \( a, b \) and functions \( F(s), G(s) \) with inverse transforms:

\[ \boxed{\; \mathcal{L}^{-1}\{aF(s) + bG(s)\} = a\,\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{F(s)\} + b\,\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{G(s)\} \; } \]

Proof. Because \( \mathcal{L} \) is linear:

\( \mathcal{L}\{a f(t) + b g(t)\} = a\,\mathcal{L}\{f(t)\} + b\,\mathcal{L}\{g(t)\} = aF(s) + bG(s). \)
Taking inverse on both sides: \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\{aF(s)+bG(s)\} = a f(t) + b g(t). \qquad \blacksquare \)
📊 4. Standard Inverse Pairs (Table 6.1)
  • \( \dfrac{1}{s} \)\( \mathcal{L}^{-1} \)\(\longrightarrow\)\( 1 \)
  • \( \dfrac{1}{s^n} \)\( \mathcal{L}^{-1} \)\(\longrightarrow\)\( \dfrac{t^{n-1}}{(n-1)!} \)
  • \( \dfrac{1}{s-a} \)\( \mathcal{L}^{-1} \)\(\longrightarrow\)\( e^{at} \)
  • \( \dfrac{1}{(s-a)^n} \)\( \mathcal{L}^{-1} \)\(\longrightarrow\)\( \dfrac{t^{n-1}e^{at}}{(n-1)!} \)
  • \( \dfrac{\omega}{s^2+\omega^2} \)\( \mathcal{L}^{-1} \)\(\longrightarrow\)\( \sin\omega t \)
  • \( \dfrac{s}{s^2+\omega^2} \)\( \mathcal{L}^{-1} \)\(\longrightarrow\)\( \cos\omega t \)
  • \( \dfrac{\omega}{s^2-\omega^2} \)\( \mathcal{L}^{-1} \)\(\longrightarrow\)\( \sinh\omega t \)
  • \( \dfrac{s}{s^2-\omega^2} \)\( \mathcal{L}^{-1} \)\(\longrightarrow\)\( \cosh\omega t \)
⚠️ CAUTION! Note that \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s^2+\omega^2}\right\} = \dfrac{\sin\omega t}{\omega} \) — the \( \omega \) in the numerator is essential. Similarly \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s^2-\omega^2}\right\} = \dfrac{\sinh\omega t}{\omega} \).
🧩 5. Partial Fractions – The Basic Idea

To invert a rational function \( \dfrac{P(s)}{Q(s)} \):

  • Ensure \( \deg P < \deg Q \) (else do polynomial division first).
  • Factorize \( Q(s) \) into linear and quadratic factors.
  • Split into partial fractions with unknown constants.
  • Solve for constants and invert each term using the standard table.

Problem 1. Find the inverse Laplace transform of \( \dfrac{3}{s^2+9} \). CO2
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Recognise \( s^2+9 = s^2+3^2 \), so \( \omega = 3 \).
Step 2: Standard form: \( \mathcal{L}\{\sin\omega t\} = \dfrac{\omega}{s^2+\omega^2} \), so \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{\omega}{s^2+\omega^2}\right\} = \sin\omega t \).
Step 3: Write \( \dfrac{3}{s^2+9} = \dfrac{3}{s^2+3^2} \).
Step 4: By linearity: \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{3}{s^2+3^2}\right\} = \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{3}{s^2+3^2}\right\} \).
Step 5: Identify \( \omega = 3 \) in the numerator, so the inverse is \( \sin 3t \).

✅ \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{3}{s^2+9}\right\} = \sin 3t \).

Problem 2. Find the inverse Laplace transform of \( \dfrac{12}{(s-3)^4} \). CO2
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Standard form: \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s^n}\right\} = \dfrac{t^{n-1}}{(n-1)!} \), and with shift \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{(s-a)^n}\right\} = \dfrac{t^{n-1}e^{at}}{(n-1)!} \).
Step 2: Here \( n = 4 \), \( a = 3 \), so \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{(s-3)^4}\right\} = \dfrac{t^{3}e^{3t}}{3!} \).
Step 3: \( 3! = 6 \), so \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{(s-3)^4}\right\} = \dfrac{t^3 e^{3t}}{6} \).
Step 4: By linearity: \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{12}{(s-3)^4}\right\} = 12 \cdot \dfrac{t^3 e^{3t}}{6} \).
Step 5: Simplify: \( = 2 t^3 e^{3t} \).

✅ \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{12}{(s-3)^4}\right\} = 2t^3 e^{3t} \).

Problem 3. Find \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{(s+1)(s+2)}\right\} \). CO2
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Partial fractions: \( \dfrac{1}{(s+1)(s+2)} = \dfrac{A}{s+1} + \dfrac{B}{s+2} \).
Step 2: Multiply by \( (s+1)(s+2) \): \( 1 = A(s+2) + B(s+1) \).
Step 3: Set \( s = -1 \): \( 1 = A(1) \Rightarrow A = 1 \).
Step 4: Set \( s = -2 \): \( 1 = B(-1) \Rightarrow B = -1 \).
Step 5: So \( \dfrac{1}{(s+1)(s+2)} = \dfrac{1}{s+1} - \dfrac{1}{s+2} \).
Step 6: Use \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s-a}\right\} = e^{at} \): \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s+1}\right\} = e^{-t} \), \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{s+2}\right\} = e^{-2t} \).
Step 7: By linearity: \( \mathcal{L}^{-1} = e^{-t} - e^{-2t} \).

✅ \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{1}{(s+1)(s+2)}\right\} = e^{-t} - e^{-2t} \).

Problem 4. Find the inverse Laplace transform of \( \dfrac{5s+1}{s^2-25} \). CO2
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Write \( s^2-25 = s^2-5^2 \), so \( \omega = 5 \).
Step 2: Split numerator: \( \dfrac{5s+1}{s^2-25} = \dfrac{5s}{s^2-25} + \dfrac{1}{s^2-25} \).
Step 3: Standard forms: \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{s}{s^2-5^2}\right\} = \cosh 5t \) and \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{5}{s^2-5^2}\right\} = \sinh 5t \).
Step 4: For the first term: \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{5s}{s^2-25}\right\} = 5\cosh 5t \).
Step 5: For the second term: \( \dfrac{1}{s^2-25} = \dfrac{1}{5}\cdot\dfrac{5}{s^2-25} \), so \( \mathcal{L}^{-1} = \dfrac{1}{5}\sinh 5t \).
Step 6: Add by linearity: \( 5\cosh 5t + \dfrac{1}{5}\sinh 5t \).

✅ \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\left\{\dfrac{5s+1}{s^2-25}\right\} = 5\cosh 5t + \dfrac{1}{5}\sinh 5t \).

📋 8. Quick Summary
  • Definition: \( f(t) = \mathcal{L}^{-1}\{F(s)\} \) if \( \mathcal{L}\{f(t)\} = F(s) \).
  • Linearity: \( \mathcal{L}^{-1}\{aF + bG\} = a\,\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{F\} + b\,\mathcal{L}^{-1}\{G\} \).
  • Standard pairs: \( 1/s \to 1 \), \( 1/s^n \to t^{n-1}/(n-1)! \), \( 1/(s-a) \to e^{at} \), \( \omega/(s^2+\omega^2) \to \sin\omega t \), \( s/(s^2+\omega^2) \to \cos\omega t \).
  • Technique: split into partial fractions, match each term to a standard pair.
