🎯 1. Learning Outcomes (CO2)
  • State the general formula for the transform of the \( n \)-th derivative \( f^{(n)}(t) \)
  • Derive the formula by induction from the first- and second-derivative results
  • Apply the formula to compute transforms by differentiation (e.g., \( \mathcal{L}\{t\cos 4t\} \), \( \mathcal{L}\{t e^{-at}\} \))
  • Use higher-order derivative theorems to obtain new transform pairs
📐 2. Theorem 2: Transform of the \( n \)-th Derivative
Laplace Transform of the Derivative \( f^{(n)} \) of Any Order

Let \( f, f', \dots, f^{(n-1)} \) be continuous for all \( t \ge 0 \) and satisfy the growth restriction; let \( f^{(n)} \) be piecewise continuous on every finite interval of \( t \ge 0 \). Then

\[ \boxed{\; \mathcal{L}\{f^{(n)}(t)\} = s^{n}F(s) - s^{n-1}f(0) - s^{n-2}f'(0) - \cdots - f^{(n-1)}(0) \; } \]

where \( F(s) = \mathcal{L}\{f(t)\} \).

Proof (by induction).

Step 1: For \( n = 1 \), the formula is \( \mathcal{L}\{f'\} = sF(s) - f(0) \), already proved.
Step 2: For \( n = 2 \), \( \mathcal{L}\{f''\} = s\,\mathcal{L}\{f'\} - f'(0) = s^2F(s) - s f(0) - f'(0) \), already proved.
Step 3: Assume the formula holds for \( n-1 \): \( \mathcal{L}\{f^{(n-1)}\} = s^{n-1}F - s^{n-2}f(0) - \cdots - f^{(n-2)}(0) \).
Step 4: Apply the first-derivative formula to \( g = f^{(n-1)} \): \( \mathcal{L}\{f^{(n)}\} = s\,\mathcal{L}\{f^{(n-1)}\} - f^{(n-1)}(0) \).
Step 5: Substituting Step 3 gives \( \mathcal{L}\{f^{(n)}\} = s^{n}F - s^{n-1}f(0) - \cdots - s f^{(n-2)}(0) - f^{(n-1)}(0) \).
Step 6: This is exactly the stated formula. Hence by induction it holds for all \( n \). \( \blacksquare \)
📊 3. Obtaining Transforms by Differentiation

A powerful application of the derivative theorem is to obtain new transforms from known ones. If \( f(0) = f'(0) = 0 \), then \( \mathcal{L}\{f''\} = s^2 \mathcal{L}\{f\} \). This lets us convert a known transform pair into a new one.

Worked illustration 1: \( \mathcal{L}\{t\sin\omega t\} \)

Let \( f(t) = t\sin\omega t \). Then \( f(0) = 0 \).
\( f'(t) = \sin\omega t + \omega t\cos\omega t \), so \( f'(0) = 0 \).
\( f''(t) = 2\omega\cos\omega t - \omega^2 t\sin\omega t \).
By Theorem 2: \( \mathcal{L}\{f''\} = s^2\mathcal{L}\{f\} - s f(0) - f'(0) = s^2\mathcal{L}\{f\} \).
Also \( \mathcal{L}\{f''\} = 2\omega\dfrac{s}{s^2+\omega^2} - \omega^2\mathcal{L}\{f\} \).
Equating: \( s^2\mathcal{L}\{f\} = 2\omega\dfrac{s}{s^2+\omega^2} - \omega^2\mathcal{L}\{f\} \).
\( (s^2+\omega^2)\mathcal{L}\{f\} = \dfrac{2\omega s}{s^2+\omega^2} \).
\( \therefore \mathcal{L}\{t\sin\omega t\} = \dfrac{2\omega s}{(s^2+\omega^2)^2} \).

Worked illustration 2: \( \mathcal{L}\{\cos\omega t\} \) and \( \mathcal{L}\{\sin\omega t\} \)

Let \( f = \cos\omega t \). Then \( f(0)=1 \), \( f'' = -\omega^2\cos\omega t \).
\( \mathcal{L}\{f''\} = s^2F - s = -\omega^2F \). So \( (s^2+\omega^2)F = s \), giving \( F = \dfrac{s}{s^2+\omega^2} \).
For \( g = \sin\omega t \): \( g(0)=0 \), \( g' = \omega\cos\omega t \).
\( \mathcal{L}\{g'\} = s\,\mathcal{L}\{g\} = \omega\dfrac{s}{s^2+\omega^2} \), so \( \mathcal{L}\{\sin\omega t\} = \dfrac{\omega}{s^2+\omega^2} \).

Problem 1. Using the derivative theorem, find \( \mathcal{L}\{t\cos 4t\} \). CO2
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Let \( f(t) = t\cos 4t \). Then \( f(0) = 0 \).
Step 2: Differentiate: \( f'(t) = \cos 4t - 4t\sin 4t \). Thus \( f'(0) = 1 \).
Step 3: Differentiate again: \( f''(t) = -4\sin 4t - 4\sin 4t - 16t\cos 4t = -8\sin 4t - 16t\cos 4t \).
Step 4: By the second-derivative theorem: \( \mathcal{L}\{f''\} = s^2\mathcal{L}\{f\} - s f(0) - f'(0) = s^2\mathcal{L}\{f\} - 1 \).
Step 5: Take transforms on the right side: \( \mathcal{L}\{-8\sin 4t - 16t\cos 4t\} = -8\cdot\dfrac{4}{s^2+16} - 16\mathcal{L}\{f\} \).
Step 6: So \( -8\cdot\dfrac{4}{s^2+16} - 16F = s^2F - 1 \).
Step 7: \( -\dfrac{32}{s^2+16} + 1 = s^2F + 16F = (s^2+16)F \).
Step 8: Left side \( = \dfrac{s^2+16-32}{s^2+16} = \dfrac{s^2-16}{s^2+16} \).
Step 9: Therefore \( F = \dfrac{s^2-16}{(s^2+16)^2} \).

✅ \( \mathcal{L}\{t\cos 4t\} = \dfrac{s^2-16}{(s^2+16)^2} \).

Problem 2. Using the derivative theorem, find \( \mathcal{L}\{t e^{-at}\} \). CO2
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: Let \( f(t) = t e^{-at} \). Then \( f(0) = 0 \).
Step 2: \( f'(t) = e^{-at} - at e^{-at} = (1 - at)e^{-at} \). Thus \( f'(0) = 1 \).
Step 3: \( f''(t) = -a e^{-at} - a e^{-at} + a^2 t e^{-at} = -2a e^{-at} + a^2 t e^{-at} \).
Step 4: By the second-derivative theorem: \( \mathcal{L}\{f''\} = s^2 F - s\cdot 0 - 1 = s^2 F - 1 \).
Step 5: On the other hand: \( \mathcal{L}\{f''\} = -2a\cdot\dfrac{1}{s+a} + a^2 F \).
Step 6: So \( s^2F - 1 = -\dfrac{2a}{s+a} + a^2F \).
Step 7: \( (s^2 - a^2)F = 1 - \dfrac{2a}{s+a} = \dfrac{s+a-2a}{s+a} = \dfrac{s-a}{s+a} \).
Step 8: \( (s^2 - a^2) = (s-a)(s+a) \), so \( F = \dfrac{1}{(s+a)^2} \).

✅ \( \mathcal{L}\{t e^{-at}\} = \dfrac{1}{(s+a)^2} \).

Problem 3. If \( \mathcal{L}\{f(t)\} = F(s) \), write down the general formula for \( \mathcal{L}\{f^{(n)}(t)\} \) and verify it for \( n = 3 \). CO2
✅ Step-by-step:
Step 1: The general formula is \( \mathcal{L}\{f^{(n)}\} = s^nF(s) - s^{n-1}f(0) - s^{n-2}f'(0) - \cdots - f^{(n-1)}(0) \).
Step 2: Apply the first-derivative theorem to \( g = f'' \): \( \mathcal{L}\{f'''\} = s\,\mathcal{L}\{f''\} - f''(0) \).
Step 3: Substitute \( \mathcal{L}\{f''\} = s^2F - s f(0) - f'(0) \).
Step 4: \( \mathcal{L}\{f'''\} = s\big(s^2F - s f(0) - f'(0)\big) - f''(0) \).
Step 5: Expand: \( \mathcal{L}\{f'''\} = s^3F - s^2 f(0) - s f'(0) - f''(0) \).

✅ This matches the general formula with \( n = 3 \).

  1. State and prove the general formula for \( \mathcal{L}\{f^{(n)}(t)\} \) by induction. (6 marks)
  2. Using the derivative theorem, find \( \mathcal{L}\{t\cos 3t\} \). (6 marks)
  3. Using the derivative theorem, find \( \mathcal{L}\{t e^{-2t}\} \). (6 marks)
  4. Obtain \( \mathcal{L}\{t\sin 2t\} \) from the second-derivative theorem. (6 marks)
  5. Let \( f(t) = \cos^2 2t \). Compute \( f(0) \), \( f'(0) \), \( f''(t) \), and use the second-derivative theorem to find \( \mathcal{L}\{\cos^2 2t\} \). (6 marks)
  6. If \( \mathcal{L}\{f(t)\} = \dfrac{1}{s^2+\omega^2} \), use the \( n \)-th derivative formula (with \( n = 2 \)) to deduce \( \mathcal{L}\{f''(t)\} \) in terms of \( s \). (6 marks)
📋 6. Quick Summary
  • General formula: \( \mathcal{L}\{f^{(n)}\} = s^nF(s) - s^{n-1}f(0) - \cdots - f^{(n-1)}(0) \)
  • Applications: obtain \( \mathcal{L}\{t\sin\omega t\} \), \( \mathcal{L}\{t\cos\omega t\} \), \( \mathcal{L}\{t e^{-at}\} \) from known transforms.
  • Key technique: choose \( f \) so that \( f(0) = f'(0) = 0 \) to simplify; then \( \mathcal{L}\{f''\} = s^2\mathcal{L}\{f\} \).
